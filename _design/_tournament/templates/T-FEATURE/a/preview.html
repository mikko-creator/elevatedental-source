MATERIAL
Plain English

What a porcelain veneer actually is

A shell of dental ceramic, typically a few tenths of a millimeter thick, bonded to the visible surface of a tooth.

It changes shape, shade, length, width and surface texture in one restoration, and it does it with a material that behaves optically like enamel: light enters it, scatters inside it, and leaves it the way it leaves a natural tooth. That is what separates a veneer that reads as a tooth from one that reads as a covering.

Each veneer is made outside the mouth by a laboratory technician working to your design, then bonded to the prepared enamel with a resin cement. The bond between ceramic and enamel is what carries the load, which is why veneers are placed on sound enamel wherever possible and why the preparation is kept conservative.

FIT
Self selection

Who porcelain veneers suit

Healthy teeth with a cosmetic problem that is stable.

Discoloration whitening cannot lift Worn or chipped edges Teeth too short for the smile Teeth too narrow for the smile Small gaps An uneven edge line A mismatched set of front teeth

They suit patients who want the most stain-resistant and hardest-wearing cosmetic option and are prepared to accept irreversible preparation and a laboratory timeline to get it.

Where they are not the right treatment.

If you want a conservative option Composite

Direct composite veneers, instead.

If you are not ready to commit to enamel reduction, direct composite veneers do a great deal in one visit with little or no cutting. That is the honest alternative and we will say so.

If your teeth are crooked Straighten first

Alignment before ceramic.

Veneers used to fake alignment mean cutting healthy tooth aggressively to fit a shell over a tooth in the wrong place. Straighten first with Invisalign (Clear Aligners) or braces, then veneer only what still needs it. Less tooth is lost.

If you grind or clench Guard first

Grinding is managed before, not after.

An unprotected ceramic edge can fracture. Grinding is assessed and managed, usually with a night guard, before veneers are considered, not after one breaks.

If the tooth has lost structure Crown

A crown is the correct restoration.

If a tooth has lost significant structure, has a large existing restoration, or has had root canal treatment, a veneer is covering only one surface of a tooth that needs full support. A crown is the correct restoration in that case, and prosthodontics sits in the same practice.

If there is decay or gum disease Treated first

Active disease is treated before anything cosmetic.

A veneer margin sitting against inflamed tissue will fail, and the tissue will not look right regardless of how good the ceramic is. Active decay and gum disease are treated first.

STEPS
Consultation to bonding

How the treatment runs

Nothing is cut until you have seen the design and agreed to it.

  1. Consultation and smile assessment, 45 to 60 minutes. Examination, photographs, and an assessment of your lip line, midline, tooth proportion and gum display. You describe what you want changed; we tell you what porcelain can and cannot do about it, and what it will cost in tooth structure.
  2. Digital records, 30 to 45 minutes. An iTero scan of both arches, a RayFace facial scan, and imaging where the underlying structure needs checking. This is the data the design is built from.
  3. Design and preview, a separate visit. A proposed design is produced and shown to you. Shape, length and shade are discussed and revised at this stage, on screen or in a trial form, while changes are still free.
  4. Preparation, 60 to 120 minutes for the set. Under local anesthetic, a small, measured amount of enamel is removed to the agreed depth. A final scan or impression is taken and sent to the laboratory with your shade prescription.
  5. Temporaries, the same appointment. Temporary veneers are fitted so you leave with functional, presentable teeth. They also let you live with the proposed shape and report back before the ceramic is finished.
  6. Laboratory stage, typically one to two weeks. The technician builds and finishes each veneer to your shade prescription and your approved design.
  7. Try-in and bonding, 90 to 120 minutes. Each veneer is tried in with a trial paste and checked for fit, margin and shade in your own face and lighting. Once you approve, the teeth are isolated and the veneers are bonded and cured.
  8. Bite check and review, 30 minutes plus a follow-up visit. Occlusion is checked and adjusted, and you return so the gum response and the bite can be reviewed after settling.

The whole course

Most cases run across two to three clinical visits over two to four weeks, plus the consultation. Your own sequence is set at the assessment, because a single tooth and a full set of visible teeth are not the same appointment count.

KIT
Named, not adjectival

The technology behind it

We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

01

RayFace facial scanner

A three-dimensional scan of your face. Veneers are designed against your own midline, lip line, smile curve and facial width, which is what stops a technically perfect set of teeth from looking wrong on the person wearing them.

02

iTero intraoral scanner

Digital impressions of both arches without impression material. The scan goes to the laboratory as data, which removes a distortion step between your mouth and the finished ceramic.

03

OccluSense bite analysis

A digital map of where your teeth contact and how hard. New ceramic edges are checked against your real bite, in force as well as position, which is the single most useful thing you can do to protect a veneer from fracturing.

04

3D CBCT imaging

Used where root position, bone level or an existing restoration needs to be understood in three dimensions before a definitive cosmetic plan is committed to.

Installed at all four clinics
05

Sol Dental Laser

Used for soft-tissue contouring where the gum line is uneven and the tooth looks short because of the tissue rather than the tooth.

06

Zoom whitening

Used before veneers where you want a lighter overall shade. Ceramic does not respond to whitening gel, so natural teeth are whitened first and the shade is allowed to settle, and only then is the ceramic made to match. The order matters.

RESULT
What porcelain does

The result, and what it is not

We do not describe your result before we have examined your teeth.

Porcelain holds its shade. Unlike composite, ceramic does not take up pigment from coffee, tea, red wine or tobacco, so a veneer looks in five years much as it looked on the day it was bonded, provided the margin and the gum around it are kept healthy. Veneers are commonly expected to serve for many years before replacement, and the variables are your bite, whether you grind, and how well the margins are maintained.

What porcelain does not do is make the tooth underneath immune to decay. The margin where ceramic meets tooth is the vulnerable line, and it is cleaned like any other tooth surface. Gum health is what keeps that line invisible.

Shade, proportion, how much can be changed and what it costs in tooth structure are decided on your case, at the assessment. That is also why there is no gallery on this page.

ASK
Nine answers, none of them hidden

Questions, answered on the page

  1. How long do porcelain veneers last? Many years with good maintenance, rather than a fixed term. Bite force, grinding, gum health and how well the margins are cleaned decide it. They are a long-term restoration that will eventually need replacing, and we say that plainly.
  2. Will my teeth be filed down? Usually yes, by a small and measured amount, and that removal cannot be undone. The tooth will need a veneer or a crown from then on. If that is not a trade you want to make, direct composite veneers are the conservative alternative and we will talk you through it.
  3. Does it hurt? Preparation is carried out under local anesthetic. Sensitivity to cold between preparation and bonding, while the temporaries are on, is common and settles once the definitive veneers are in place. Tell your clinician about any sensitivity during that period.
  4. How many do I need? Only as many as the smile needs. Some cases are a single tooth, others span the teeth visible when you smile widely. Treating fewer teeth is better dentistry when fewer teeth is what the case calls for, and we will not propose a set of ten to make a plan look complete.
  5. Can veneers be whitened later? No. Ceramic does not respond to whitening gel. If you want a lighter overall shade, whiten your natural teeth first and let the shade settle, then have the veneers made to match. The order matters.
  6. What happens if one chips or comes off? Come in. A debonded veneer can often be re-bonded if it is intact; a fractured one is generally remade rather than repaired, because ceramic does not patch the way composite does.
  7. Porcelain or composite? Porcelain is harder, holds its shade, is laboratory-made, takes two to three visits, requires irreversible preparation and starts higher. Composite is one visit, conservative, repairable chairside and starts lower, and it stains and chips more readily. The table directly above this list sets the two out point by point.
  8. Which clinic can I book? We have four clinics in Metro Manila: Mitsukoshi BGC, Shangri-La Plaza, Bonifacio Stopover BGC and Alabang Town Center. Their addresses, direct lines and individual opening hours are on the locations page and in the header of every page on this site.
  9. Aftercare. Treat your temporaries carefully: soft food, no flossing straight up and out at the temporary margin, and call us if one comes loose rather than waiting. Once the definitive veneers are bonded, clean them exactly as you would enamel, with daily flossing at the margins, because gum health at that line is what keeps the result invisible. Do not use your front teeth as tools. No ice, no nails, no opening packaging. If you grind at night, wear the guard every night. Keep your recall appointments so margins and gum levels are checked before a small problem becomes a remake.
