Go to stage 01
01
Stage 01, plain English

What a porcelain veneer is

A porcelain veneer is a shell of dental ceramic, typically a few tenths of a millimeter thick, bonded to the visible surface of a tooth.

It changes shape, shade, length, width and surface texture in one restoration, and it does it with a material that behaves optically like enamel: light enters it, scatters inside it, and leaves it the way it leaves a natural tooth. That is what separates a veneer that reads as a tooth from one that reads as a covering.

We use Emax, a pressed lithium disilicate ceramic. It is chosen because it is strong enough to be made thin and translucent enough to be layered for a natural edge, which is the combination cosmetic cases need.

Each veneer is made outside the mouth by a laboratory technician working to your design, then bonded to the prepared enamel with a resin cement. The bond between ceramic and enamel is what carries the load, which is why veneers are placed on sound enamel wherever possible and why the preparation is kept conservative.

The honest part

Placing porcelain veneers usually requires removing a small amount of enamel so the shell sits flush rather than proud. That removal is irreversible. Once a tooth is prepared it will need a veneer or a crown on it from then on. This is the trade you are making for the durability and stain resistance porcelain gives you, and it is the first thing we discuss, not the last.

Stage 02, self selection

Who porcelain veneers suit, and where the route forks

Porcelain veneers suit healthy teeth with a cosmetic problem that is stable.

Discoloration whitening cannot lift Worn or chipped edges Teeth too short or too narrow Small gaps An uneven edge line A mismatched set of front teeth

They suit patients who want the most stain-resistant and hardest-wearing cosmetic option and are prepared to accept irreversible preparation and a laboratory timeline to get it. If that is your case, the route continues at stage 03. If one of the four turnings below describes you, take it, and the treatment named on it is the one to read next.

Turning 01 Conservative

Direct composite veneers

If you want a conservative option, or you are not ready to commit to enamel reduction, direct composite veneers do a great deal in one visit with little or no cutting. That is the honest alternative and we will say so.

Composite veneers
Turning 02 Move first

Straighten first, then veneer

If your teeth are significantly crooked, rotated or crowded, veneers used to fake alignment mean cutting healthy tooth aggressively to fit a shell over a tooth that is in the wrong place. Straighten first with Invisalign (Clear Aligners) or braces, then veneer only what still needs it. The result is better and less tooth is lost.

Clear aligners Braces
Turning 03 Protect first

A night guard, before ceramic

If you grind or clench, an unprotected ceramic edge can fracture. Grinding is assessed and managed, usually with a night guard, before veneers are considered, not after one breaks.

General dentistry
Turning 04 Full support

A crown, not a veneer

If a tooth has lost significant structure, has a large existing restoration, or has had root canal treatment, a veneer is covering only one surface of a tooth that needs full support. A crown is the correct restoration in that case.

Dental crowns

Before any of the four

Active decay and gum disease are treated first. A veneer margin sitting against inflamed tissue will fail, and the tissue will not look right regardless of how good the ceramic is. That is a precondition of every path on this page, not a fifth alternative to weigh against the others.

Stage 03, consultation to bite check

How the treatment runs

Eight steps, in this order, with the time each one takes at the chair.

Nothing is cut until you have seen a design and agreed to it, and nothing is bonded until you have seen the finished ceramic in your own face and lighting. Those two gates are the reason the sequence has as many steps as it does.

The eight clinical steps
StepWhat happensHow long
01 Consultation and smile assessment Examination, photographs, and an assessment of your lip line, midline, tooth proportion and gum display. You describe what you want changed. We tell you what porcelain can and cannot do about it, and what it will cost in tooth structure. 45 to 60 minutes
02 Digital records An iTero scan of both arches, a RayFace facial scan, and imaging where the underlying structure needs checking. This is the data the design is built from. 30 to 45 minutes
03 Design and preview A proposed design is produced and shown to you. Shape, length and shade are discussed and revised at this stage, on screen or in a trial form, while changes are still free. A separate visit
04 Preparation Under local anesthetic, a small, measured amount of enamel is removed to the agreed depth. A final scan or impression is taken and sent to the laboratory with your shade prescription. 60 to 120 minutes for the set
05 Temporaries Temporary veneers are fitted so you leave with functional, presentable teeth. They also let you live with the proposed shape and report back before the ceramic is finished. Same appointment
06 Laboratory stage The technician builds and finishes each veneer to your approved design and shade prescription. Typically one to two weeks
07 Try-in and bonding Each veneer is tried in with a trial paste and checked for fit, margin and shade in your own face and lighting. Once you approve, the teeth are isolated and the veneers are bonded and cured. 90 to 120 minutes
08 Bite check and review Occlusion is checked and adjusted, and you return so the gum response and the bite can be reviewed after settling. 30 minutes, plus a follow-up visit

Your calendar

Most cases run across two to three clinical visits over two to four weeks, plus the consultation.

Stage 04, the bench

Who performs it

Porcelain veneers are performed by our Cosmetic Dentistry specialists, working with prosthodontics on the restorative design.

Where the case needs orthodontic movement first, or gum contouring to correct an uneven gum line, those specialists sit in the same group. Your case is planned and completed in house, so the design is not handed between practices halfway through.

Every clinician who works on your case is listed by name, discipline and training on our specialists page.

Cosmetic Dentistry

The discipline that owns the case. Shade, shape, proportion, the preparation depth and the bonding itself are decided and carried out here.

Prosthodontics

Works with cosmetic dentistry on the restorative design, and takes the case outright where a tooth needs full coverage rather than a shell on one surface.

Orthodontics

Where teeth have to be moved before anything is veneered, the movement is planned here first, with Invisalign or braces, and the ceramic follows the alignment.

Periodontics

Where the gum line is uneven or the tissue is inflamed, the gum is treated and contoured here before any margin is placed against it.

The record chain

Between your face and the finished ceramic, your case passes through six named stations.

Station 01

RayFace facial scanner

A three-dimensional scan of your face. Veneers are designed against your own midline, lip line, smile curve and facial width, which is what stops a technically correct set of teeth from looking wrong on the person wearing them.

Stage 07, what porcelain does

The result, and what it is not

We do not describe your result before we have examined your teeth.

Porcelain holds its shade. Unlike composite, ceramic does not take up pigment from coffee, tea, red wine or tobacco, so a veneer looks in five years much as it looked on the day it was bonded, provided the margin and the gum around it are kept healthy.

Veneers are commonly expected to serve for many years before replacement, and the variables are your bite, whether you grind, and how well the margins are maintained.

What porcelain does not do is make the tooth underneath immune to decay. The margin where ceramic meets tooth is the vulnerable line, and it is cleaned like any other tooth surface. Gum health is what keeps that line invisible.

Shade, proportion, how much can be changed and what it costs you in tooth structure are decided on your own case, at the assessment.

Stage 08, eight answers

Questions, in the order they come up

Four questions people ask before they agree to anything, and four they ask once the ceramic is in.

None of them is hidden behind a form and none of them is answered with a number this page cannot support.

Cosmetic Dentistry Treatment Prices

Before a tooth is prepared

Will my teeth be filed down?

Usually yes, by a small and measured amount, and that removal cannot be undone. The tooth will need a veneer or a crown from then on. If that is not a trade you want to make, direct composite veneers are the conservative alternative and we will talk you through it.

How many do I need?

Only as many as the smile needs. Some cases are a single tooth, others span the teeth visible when you smile widely. Treating fewer teeth is better dentistry when fewer teeth is what the case calls for, and we will not propose a set of ten to make a plan look complete.

Porcelain or composite?

Porcelain is harder, holds its shade, is laboratory made, takes two to three visits, requires irreversible preparation and has a higher starting price. Composite is one visit, conservative, repairable chairside and has a lower starting price, and it stains and chips more readily. The right answer depends on your teeth, your bite, your timeline and how much tooth structure you are willing to give up.

Which clinic can I book?

We have four clinics in Metro Manila: Mitsukoshi BGC, Shangri-La Plaza, Bonifacio Stopover BGC and Alabang Town Center. Which of them schedule this treatment, and on which days, is being confirmed with the practice, so book through the appointment control and you will be placed at the branch that runs your case.

During treatment and after

Does it hurt?

Preparation is carried out under local anesthetic. Sensitivity to cold between preparation and bonding, while the temporaries are on, is common and settles once the definitive veneers are in place. Tell your clinician about any sensitivity during that period.

How long do porcelain veneers last?

Many years with good maintenance, rather than a fixed term. Bite force, grinding, gum health and how well the margins are cleaned decide it. They are a long-term restoration that will eventually need replacing, and we say that plainly.

Can veneers be whitened later?

No. Ceramic does not respond to whitening gel. If you want a lighter overall shade, whiten your natural teeth first and let the shade settle, then have the veneers made to match. The order matters.

What happens if one chips or comes off?

Come in. A debonded veneer can often be re-bonded if it is intact, and a fractured one is generally remade rather than repaired, because ceramic does not patch the way composite does. Replacement and remake terms are pending the practice's written policy and are not stated on this page until they are supplied.

Stage 09, the end of the route

Aftercare, and what a first appointment gives you

Ceramic is looked after the way enamel is looked after, with two additions and one habit to drop.

  • Treat your temporaries carefully: soft food, no flossing straight up and out at the temporary margin, and call us if one comes loose rather than waiting.
  • Once the definitive veneers are bonded, clean them exactly as you would enamel, with daily flossing at the margins, because gum health at that line is what keeps the result invisible.
  • Do not use your front teeth as tools. No ice, no nails, no opening packaging.
  • If you grind at night, wear the guard every night.
  • Keep your recall appointments so margins and gum levels are checked before a small problem becomes a remake.

A first appointment produces three things: an examination of your teeth and gums against what you want changed, a design shown to you against your own facial proportions, and a written per-tooth quote. None of them commits you to treatment, and none of them requires a tooth to be prepared first.
