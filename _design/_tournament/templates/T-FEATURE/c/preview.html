The case for the ceramic

What you are paying the extra for

A shell of dental ceramic, typically a few tenths of a millimeter thick, bonded to the visible surface of a tooth. It changes shape, shade, length, width and surface texture in one restoration.

Each veneer is made outside the mouth by a laboratory technician working to your design, then bonded to the prepared enamel with a resin cement. That is the difference the price is buying: a restoration built under laboratory conditions rather than sculpted freehand against a patient's lip in a single appointment.

The specification

Material
Emax, pressed lithium disilicate
Thickness

Carries the load
The ceramic to enamel bond

How it behaves in light

Light enters the ceramic, scatters inside it, and leaves it the way it leaves a natural tooth. That is what separates a veneer that reads as a tooth from one that reads as a covering, and it is the single property composite cannot match.

Why Emax specifically

It is chosen because it is strong enough to be made thin and translucent enough to be layered for a natural edge. Cosmetic cases need both at once, and a material that gives you only one of them forces a compromise into the result.

What carries the load

The bond between ceramic and enamel, not the shell itself. That is why veneers are placed on sound enamel wherever possible, and why the preparation is kept conservative rather than generous.

05/10
Self selection

Five routes that are not this one

Porcelain veneers suit healthy teeth with a cosmetic problem that is stable: discoloration that whitening cannot lift, worn or chipped edges, teeth too short or too narrow for the smile, small gaps, an uneven edge line, or a mismatched set of front teeth. Everything below is a case where the answer is somewhere else, and each one links to it.

If you want a conservative option

Direct composite veneers

If you are not ready to commit to enamel reduction, direct composite veneers do a great deal in one visit with little or no cutting. That is the honest alternative and we will say so.

Cosmetic Dentistry
If your teeth are crooked or crowded

Straighten first, then veneer what is left

Veneers used to fake alignment mean cutting healthy tooth aggressively to fit a shell over a tooth that is in the wrong place. Straighten first with Invisalign (Clear Aligners) or braces, then veneer only what still needs it. The result is better and less tooth is lost.

Invisalign Braces
If you grind or clench

Grinding is managed before, not after

An unprotected ceramic edge can fracture. Grinding is assessed and managed, usually with a night guard, before veneers are considered, not after one breaks.

General Dentistry
If the tooth has lost structure

A crown, not a veneer

If a tooth has lost significant structure, has a large existing restoration, or has had root canal treatment, a veneer covers only one surface of a tooth that needs full support. A crown is the correct restoration in that case, and prosthodontics sits in the same practice.

Prosthodontics
If there is decay or gum disease

Active disease is treated first

A veneer margin sitting against inflamed tissue will fail, and the tissue will not look right regardless of how good the ceramic is. Active decay and gum disease are treated before anything cosmetic begins.

If you take this route

How the treatment runs

Nothing is cut until you have seen the design and agreed to it.

  1. Consultation and smile assessment, 45 to 60 minutes. Examination, photographs, and an assessment of your lip line, midline, tooth proportion and gum display. You say what you want changed; we say what porcelain can and cannot do about it, and what it costs in tooth structure.
  2. Digital records, 30 to 45 minutes. An iTero scan of both arches, a RayFace facial scan, and imaging where the underlying structure needs checking. This is the data the design is built from.
  3. Design and preview, a separate visit. A proposed design is produced and shown to you. Shape, length and shade are discussed and revised at this stage, on screen or in a trial form, while changes are still free.
  4. Preparation, 60 to 120 minutes for the set. Under local anesthetic, a small, measured amount of enamel is removed to the agreed depth. A final scan or impression is taken and sent to the laboratory with your shade prescription.
  5. Temporaries, the same appointment. Temporary veneers are fitted so you leave with functional, presentable teeth. They also let you live with the proposed shape and report back before the ceramic is finished.
  6. Laboratory stage, typically one to two weeks. The technician builds and finishes each veneer to your shade prescription and your approved design.
  7. Try-in and bonding, 90 to 120 minutes. Each veneer is tried in with a trial paste and checked for fit, margin and shade in your own lighting. Once you approve, they are bonded and cured.
  8. Bite check and review, 30 minutes plus a follow-up. Occlusion is checked and adjusted, and you return so the gum response and the bite are reviewed after settling.
Where the choice is still open Three points

You can still change your mind after step three.

The sequence above is not a conveyor. Three of its eight stages exist so the decision can be revisited while it is still cheap to revisit, and the only irreversible one is step four.

Who decides, and with what

The instruments that settle the argument

Each of these answers one question the choice depends on. We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

01

RayFace facial scanner

Does the proposed shape fit your face? A three-dimensional scan of your face, so veneers are designed against your own midline, lip line, smile curve and facial width. That is what stops a technically perfect set of teeth from looking wrong on the person wearing them.

02

iTero intraoral scanner

What does the laboratory work to? Digital impressions of both arches without impression material. The scan goes to the laboratory as data, which removes a distortion step between your mouth and the finished ceramic.

03

OccluSense bite analysis

Will your bite fracture a ceramic edge? A digital map of where your teeth contact and how hard. New edges are checked against your real bite, in force as well as position, which is the single most useful thing that can be done to protect a veneer.

04

3D CBCT imaging

Is this a veneer case at all? Installed at all four clinics. Used where root position, bone level or an existing restoration needs to be understood in three dimensions before a definitive cosmetic plan is committed to.

Porcelain veneers are performed by our Cosmetic Dentistry specialists, working with prosthodontics on the restorative design. Where the case needs orthodontic movement first, or gum contouring to correct an uneven gum line, those specialists sit in the same group. Your case is planned and completed in-house, so the design is not handed between practices halfway through.

Nine answers

Questions, grouped by where you are in the decision

Still choosing

Porcelain or composite?

Porcelain: harder, holds its shade, laboratory-made, two to three visits, requires irreversible preparation, higher starting price. Composite: one visit, conservative, repairable chairside, lower starting price, stains and chips more readily.

The right answer depends on your teeth, your bite, your timeline and how much tooth structure you are willing to give up.

Will my teeth be filed down?

Usually yes, by a small and measured amount, and that removal cannot be undone. The tooth will need a veneer or a crown from then on.

If that is not a trade you want to make, direct composite veneers are the conservative alternative and we will talk you through it.

How long do porcelain veneers last?

Many years with good maintenance, rather than a fixed term. Bite force, grinding, gum health and how well the margins are cleaned decide it.

They are a long-term restoration that will eventually need replacing, and we say that plainly.

How many do I need?

Only as many as the smile needs. Some cases are a single tooth, others span the teeth visible when you smile widely.

Treating fewer teeth is better dentistry when fewer teeth is what the case calls for; we will not propose a set of ten to make a plan look complete.

Once you have decided

Does it hurt?

Preparation is carried out under local anesthetic. Sensitivity to cold between preparation and bonding, while the temporaries are on, is common and settles once the definitive veneers are in place.

Tell your clinician about any sensitivity during that period.

Can veneers be whitened later?

No. Ceramic does not respond to whitening gel. If you want a lighter overall shade, whiten your natural teeth first and let the shade settle, then have the veneers made to match.

The order matters, and it is one of the things decided at the design stage rather than after bonding.

What happens if one chips or comes off?

Come in. A debonded veneer can often be re-bonded if it is intact; a fractured one is generally remade rather than repaired, because ceramic does not patch the way composite does.

Replacement and remake terms are pending the client's written policy and are not stated on this page until they exist.

Practical

How do I look after them?

Treat your temporaries carefully: soft food, no flossing straight up and out at the temporary margin, and call us if one comes loose rather than waiting.

Once the definitive veneers are bonded, clean them exactly as you would enamel, with daily flossing at the margins, because gum health at that line is what keeps the result invisible. Do not use your front teeth as tools: no ice, no nails, no opening packaging. If you grind at night, wear the guard every night.

Keep your recall appointments so margins and gum levels are checked before a small problem becomes a remake.

Which clinic can I book?

We have four clinics in Metro Manila: Mitsukoshi BGC, Shangri-La Plaza, Bonifacio Stopover BGC and Alabang Town Center. Their direct lines and their opening hours are on the rail at the top of every page.

The consultation

What the consultation decides

Three things, in this order.

  1. Which route your case belongs to. Porcelain, direct composite, or one of the five treatments that come first. This is settled by examination, with the scans and the bite map, not by preference.
  2. A design you see before anything is cut. Produced from an iTero scan of both arches and a RayFace facial scan, and revised while revisions are still free.
  3. A written per-tooth quote. Confirmed at consultation, before any tooth is prepared, against the case we have actually examined.

If porcelain is not the right treatment for your teeth, you will be told that at the consultation, and you will be told what is.
