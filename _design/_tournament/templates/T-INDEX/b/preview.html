You are at stop 02 of 05

A short pinned set sits above the full list. It is not a ranking and it is not the whole roster.

It is a small, deliberately chosen group, kept current, so that a first-time visitor who does not yet know whether their case is prosthodontic or periodontic still lands on a real clinician rather than an empty filter box.

Each pinned card carries the same information as every other card on this page. Nothing in the pinned set is presented that is not also true of the person in the list below.

If none of them matches your case, scroll. The full roster is directly underneath, and the filters are faster than reading.

Four pinned slots, reserved

What each pinned card will carry, once the four names land.

  1. A photograph. The same roster crop every other card on this page uses, so the pinned set reads as part of the bench rather than a separate advertisement.
  2. The clinician's name. Exactly as it is published on their own profile.
  3. Credentials, as published. Copied from the profile, never expanded, abbreviated or re-ordered by the page.
  4. The field they practice in. The same value the roster card carries, so a pinned clinician and a listed clinician are never described differently.
  5. A link to the full profile. Plus one sentence approved by that clinician, which is the only thing a pinned card holds that a roster card does not.
Owner data, held

The featured selection. Four slots are reserved and the copy around them is final, but the selection itself is an owner decision and has not been supplied. It may not be auto-filled from roster order, alphabetical order or recency, and it may not be inferred from the profile pages. Each slot needs a name, credentials as published, a field, and one sentence approved by that clinician. Until then this band states the contract and shows no face, because a pinned card built from a guess is a recommendation the practice never made.

What the profile adds

  1. Training. Where the clinician qualified and what they went on to study, which is the part of a specialist claim that is checkable rather than asserted.
  2. The treatments that clinician performs. Named, so you can match your own case to a person rather than to a department.
  3. The discipline hub their work belongs to. Which is where the field is explained, the treatments inside it are listed, and what those treatments start at is shown.

Read it before you book. A specialist page that tells you nothing beyond a name is not doing its job, and neither is a roster you cannot narrow.

Routing

If the filter comes back empty, go sideways.

A filter that returns nothing must say so in words. It never renders as a blank panel, and it never leaves you at a dead end with no way out except the back button.

Three routes sit beneath the list, in this order.

Open the hub for the field.

End of the route

You do not have to pick the right specialist yourself.

If you know whose name you want, book that clinician directly from their profile. If you do not, book the consultation and the case is routed internally to the field it belongs to. Either way a specialist looks at the actual case, and you leave with a written quote before anything is scheduled.
