The roster

Dental Specialists in Manila. Meet our 31 dentists.

Thirty-one published clinician profiles, across nine disciplines and four Metro Manila clinics. This is the roster, not a selection from it. Filter it down to the field your case sits in, read the profile of the person who would treat you, and book that appointment specifically. You are choosing a clinician here, not a building.

Book an Appointment Compare the nine fields
Before you choose

Two things this page will not do. It will not put a shortlist in front of the roster, because the pinned selection is an owner decision and has not been supplied. It will not tell you which clinic a named clinician sits in, because the per clinic schedules are not confirmed and no row on this page carries a clinic value.

Route A

You choose the clinician.

Narrow the roster to the field your case sits in, read the profile of the person who would treat you, and book that clinician directly from their profile. If you already know whose name you want, this is the shorter route and nobody stands between you and it.

The cost of this route

You have to know which field your case belongs to. A case that turns out to span two fields is not a thing the person carrying it can reliably sort, and the filter cannot sort it for you either.

Route B

You let the case be routed.

Book the consultation and the case is routed internally to the field it belongs to. Complex work is not meant to be sorted by the patient, and a case that turns out to span two fields is handled by two of our own specialists rather than sent out to a third party.

The cost of this route

You do not pick the name. You get the field the case belongs to, chosen by the group, and you meet that clinician at the consultation rather than before it.

02
Where to start

A place to start, if you do not know which field you need.

A short pinned set sits above the full list. It is not a ranking and it is not the whole roster. It is a small, deliberately chosen group, kept current, so that a first-time visitor who does not yet know whether their case is prosthodontic or periodontic still lands on a real clinician rather than an empty filter box.

Each pinned card carries the same information as every other card on this page: a photograph, the clinician's name, their credentials exactly as published, the field they practice in, and a link to the full profile. Nothing in the pinned set is presented that is not also true of the person in the list below.

Owner data, held

Four pinned slots are reserved and the copy above is final, but the selection itself is an owner decision and has not been supplied. It is not filled in here from roster order, alphabetical order or recency, and it is not inferred from the profile pages. Each slot needs a name, credentials as published, a field, and one sentence approved by that clinician.

Pinned slots reserved, 4 Selection, an owner decision Shown on this page, none

Until the four names land, the honest place to start is the comparison directly below. Read the nine fields, find the one your case sits in, and take Route A. If none of them is obviously yours, that is not a failure of reading, it is what Route B is for.

03
Filter the roster

The full roster.

Every published profile appears below. The list holds 31 specialists, and the count on this page and the count on the profile set are the same number by design: this page shows the bench, all of it, without a shortlist standing in front of it.

The profile is where the per-person detail sits: training, the treatments that clinician performs, and the discipline hub their work belongs to. Read it before you book. A specialist page that tells you nothing beyond a name is not doing its job, and neither is a roster you cannot narrow.

Until every profile's field and credential string is confirmed, the roster is presented by the field it sorts into. Nine rows, one per field, each carrying the treatments inside it, the boundary where it stops, and the route through to the clinicians who practise it. The toolbar filters those rows on values the rows actually carry, and on nothing else.

Nine fields, what each one covers, and where each one stops
Field What it covers Where it stops Read next
General Dentistry Examination, Dental Fillings, Oral Prophylaxis, AirFlow (guided biofilm therapy) and Dental Sealant, plus the records that go with them. At work that belongs to a named field. A canal, a gum, a surgical extraction or an arch that has to move is handled by the field it belongs to. The General Dentistry hub
Cosmetic Dentistry How a tooth reads: shade, shape and surface. Porcelain Veneers, Zirconia Veneers, Composite (Direct) Veneers and Laser Teeth Whitening. At appearance. It does not straighten an arch, which is orthodontic, and it does not replace a tooth that is gone, which is prosthodontic. The Cosmetic Dentistry hub
Orthodontics The position of the teeth you still have. Invisalign (Clear Aligners) and Braces, planned before the first appliance is made. At movement. A missing tooth is not an orthodontic problem, and a tooth that hurts is assessed and treated before anything is moved. The Orthodontics hub
Oral Surgery What has to come out or go in: surgical extraction, and the placement of a Dental Implant in bone. At the surgery. The crown that goes on top of an implant is prosthodontic work, and it is planned before the placement rather than after it. The Oral Surgery hub
Pediatric Dentistry Children. Fluoride Treatment, Dental Sealant, Oral Prophylaxis, and the first appointments a child ever has. This is the one field defined by the patient rather than the procedure. An adult with the same need is treated in the matching adult field. The Pediatric Dentistry hub
Prosthodontics Replacing and rebuilding. Dental Crowns, Dental Bridge, Complete Dentures, Removable Partial Dentures, and the restoration that sits on an implant. At the rebuild. It does not treat the infection inside the tooth or the gum that let the tooth fail, and both are settled first. The Prosthodontics hub
Endodontics The inside of the tooth. Root Canal Treatment, and the diagnosis that decides whether a tooth can be kept at all. At the root. What protects the tooth afterwards is usually a crown, and a crown is prosthodontic. The Endodontics hub
Periodontics The foundation. The gum and the bone the teeth stand in, and Tooth Scaling and Root Planing where they are inflamed. At the foundation. A tooth already lost is replaced in another field, and the replacement waits until the foundation is stable. The Periodontics hub
Digital Dental X-rays The record. Digital Dental X-ray, and 3D CBCT imaging, which is available at all four clinics. Imaging is not treatment. It tells the other eight fields what they are looking at, before anyone touches anything. The Digital Dental X-rays hub

  1. Photograph. One portrait treatment, one crop ratio, one background handling, applied to every profile. Sixty portrait files exist across the thirty-one profiles and they were not shot to one standard.
  2. Name. As published on the profile. This is the value the A to Z sort reads.
  3. Credentials. Exactly as published, confirmed string by string. None of the three above is confirmed, so none of the three shows one.
  4. Field. The discipline the profile sorts into. This is the value the discipline chips read, and it is held per profile until the owner confirms it.
  5. Clinic. The row that is deliberately absent. Per clinic assignment is not confirmed, and a named clinician printed at a named address on a medical site is not a gap, it is an error.
  6. Profile link. The route out of the listing and into the detail, which is where training and the treatments that clinician performs actually live.
