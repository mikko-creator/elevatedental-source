Stage 01, the object and the material

What a crown is, and what it is cut from

A crown covers the whole visible part of a tooth. The tooth is reduced by a controlled amount on every surface, a copy of it goes to the laboratory, and the finished cap is cemented over what remains.

Once it is on, the cap takes the biting force the weakened tooth could no longer take safely. That is the entire mechanism. There is nothing clever about it and nothing hidden in it.

The problem it solves is a tooth that is still yours but is no longer strong enough to be left as it is. A large old filling, a crack, a fractured cusp, heavy wear, or the hollowed out structure left behind after root canal treatment all put a tooth at risk of splitting. A cap is what prevents the split, and a split root usually cannot be saved.

Material is chosen for the position and the load, never as an upsell. Your prosthodontist names the material for your case and the reason for it before the tooth is touched.

The honest part

Preparing a tooth for full coverage removes sound enamel and dentine on every surface, and that removal is irreversible. From then on the tooth needs a cap on it. That is the trade you make for keeping a tooth that would otherwise split, and it is the first thing we discuss, not the last.

Material 01

Zirconia

The strongest of the tooth coloured options and the usual choice for molars, and for anyone who grinds. It takes the load a back tooth actually sees. It is the least translucent of the four, which matters at the front and matters very little behind the canine.

Load bearing positions
Material 02

Lithium disilicate, sold as Emax

The usual choice at the front. It lets light through the way enamel does, so it is easier to match to the tooth standing next to it. That optical behaviour is the reason it is specified, not a preference for one brand over another.

Visible positions
Material 03

Porcelain fused to metal

Still valid in particular cases, and still specified where a metal substructure earns its place. Its limitation is the opaque core, which can read as a grey line at the gum if the tissue recedes over the years.

Case by case
Material 04

Gold

Unfashionable and still, on a second molar nobody sees, mechanically excellent. It can be made thinner than ceramic, which means less tooth is cut away to make room for it. We will offer it where it is the better answer and we will say why.

Who a cap suits, and where the route forks

Full coverage suits a tooth that is heavily broken down, cracked, worn, or freshly root treated, and it suits a back tooth doing the chewing before it suits a front one.

A large old filling with little left around it A cracked cusp Heavy wear across the biting surface A tooth whose nerve has been treated The support tooth at either end of a bridge The restoration seated on an implant

If that describes your tooth, the route continues at stage 03. If one of the four turnings below describes it instead, take the turning: the treatment named on it is the one to read next, and it is the one we will recommend at the chair.

Turning 01 Less is correct

A filling, an inlay or an onlay

If the damage is small, full coverage removes more healthy tooth than the problem justifies. A direct filling, or a laboratory made inlay or onlay covering only the part that failed, is the more conservative treatment and the right one.

Dental fillings
Turning 02 Nothing to grip

A post and core, or replacement

If too little tooth stands above the gum, a cap has nothing to hold on to. The honest options are a post and core where the root is sound, or removal and replacement where it is not. We will tell you which at the assessment.

Prosthodontics Dental implants
Turning 03 Cannot be rescued

A vertical root fracture

A tooth split down the root, or one whose bone support has gone to advanced gum disease, is not rescued by capping it. We say that at the assessment rather than after you have paid for one, and we plan the replacement instead.

Periodontics
Turning 04 More than one tooth

A bridge, spanning the gap

Where a tooth is already missing and the teeth on either side are sound enough to carry it, the caps become the abutments of a bridge rather than a single unit. That is a different plan, a different price and a different page.

Dental bridge

Before any of the four

Active decay and untreated gum inflammation are dealt with first, always. A margin sitting against inflamed tissue leaks, and the tissue will not settle around it however good the ceramic is. Read that as a condition on all four turnings above rather than as a fifth one competing with them.

Stage 03, assessment to review

How the treatment runs, and what is checked before anything is cemented

Seven steps, in this order, and the minutes each one occupies at the chair.

Most cases finish in two appointments across one to two weeks. A tooth needing nerve treatment or a core build up first takes longer, and the plan you are handed at the assessment states the sequence for your case specifically rather than for a typical one.

The running order, step by step
StepWhat happensHow long
01 Consultation and assessment Clinical examination, radiographs, and a reading of how much sound tooth remains, the state of the nerve and the condition of the gum. Material and shade are discussed. The written quote is handed over at this appointment. 45 to 60 minutes
02 Preliminary treatment, where needed Removal of decay, replacement of a failing filling, a core build up, or nerve treatment. Each is quoted separately and completed before the restoration itself is started. A separate visit
03 Preparation and records The tooth is numbed and shaped. The arch is scanned or impressed, the bite is recorded, and the shade is matched under controlled light rather than under the chair lamp. 60 to 90 minutes
04 Temporary cover A provisional unit protects the prepared tooth and holds the gum in position while the laboratory works. It is cemented lightly on purpose so it lifts off without damaging the preparation. Same appointment
05 Laboratory fabrication The unit is built to the scan, the bite record and the shade prescription sent with it. This step sets the calendar for the case, not the chair time. One to two weeks
06 Fitting Margin, contacts, shade and bite are read before any cement is mixed. Anything that is not right is corrected, or the unit goes back to the bench rather than being cemented as it is. 45 minutes
07 Review The bite is rechecked after you have actually chewed on it, and the gum margin is assessed once the tissue has settled around the new edge. One short visit, two to four weeks later

The four checks at step 06

  • The margin is read with a probe, and on a radiograph wherever the edge sits below the gum where a probe cannot see.
  • The contacts against the neighbouring teeth are tested with floss, because a contact that is open traps food and one that is tight will not let you clean.
  • The shade is judged in daylight, not under the chair lamp, which flatters everything.
  • The bite is read in position and in force before cement is mixed, and again at the review once you have used it.

Stage 04, the bench and the instruments

Who performs it, and what reads your bite

Crowns are prosthodontics, the recognised specialty concerned with restoring and replacing teeth, and it is practised in house here as a main clinical discipline rather than as a sideline.

When a case turns out to need nerve treatment first, or gum treatment to establish a clean edge, that work is done by a specialist inside the same practice. Your case is not handed to another clinic halfway through and handed back with a different plan.

We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

Our Specialists Prosthodontics
System 01

iTero intraoral scanner

A digital scan of the shaped tooth, the opposing arch and the bite, taken in the chair. It removes the impression tray for most cases, shows the preparation magnified on screen so an undercut is caught before you leave, and sends the file to the laboratory the same day.

Named installed system
System 02

OccluSense bite analysis

A unit sitting a fraction high is the most common reason a newly restored tooth aches. OccluSense records where each contact lands and how hard, in sequence, so the adjustment is made against a reading rather than by feel alone.

Named installed system
System 03

3D CBCT imaging

Used where a two dimensional radiograph cannot answer the question: root anatomy before nerve treatment, a suspected crack, the extent of bone support, or planning a restoration seated on an implant.

Your own figure is written for you at the free consultation, once the tooth has been assessed. Confirm current consultation terms with the clinic before you book.

What moves the final figure

  • Material. Zirconia, lithium disilicate and porcelain fused to metal are priced differently, and the difference is the material and the bench time, not a tier of service.
  • Number of units. Restorations are priced per tooth, so a case rebuilding several teeth scales with the count rather than with a package.
  • Preliminary treatment. Endodontic treatment, a post and core, or a core build up are each quoted separately and none is folded into the headline.
  • Position in the arch. A front tooth carries a shade and translucency requirement a molar does not, and that adds clinical and laboratory time.
  • Case complexity. A deep margin, a difficult shade match, or a bite that has to be rebuilt across several teeth all add time at the chair and at the bench.

The exact figure is confirmed in writing after the clinical assessment and the radiographs. We do not price a tooth we have not seen.

Stage 06, the end of the route

Questions, aftercare, and what a first appointment gives you

Eight answers, none of them hidden behind a form and none of them carrying a number this page cannot support.

No warranty, free replacement window or durability commitment is stated on this page for this treatment.

Will it hurt?

The preparation appointment is carried out under local anaesthetic. A tooth with a live nerve is commonly sensitive to cold for a few days afterwards while the temporary is in place, and that usually settles once the definitive unit is cemented. Sensitivity that is getting worse rather than better is worth a call, not a wait.

How long does the temporary stay on?

One to two weeks. Avoid sticky and very hard food on that side while it is there. If it comes off, keep it and telephone the clinic rather than leaving the shaped surface uncovered, because the tooth can drift and the gum can close over the edge in a surprisingly short time.

How long will it last?

Longevity depends on the bite, the amount of sound tooth left underneath, your hygiene and whether you grind. Restorations of this kind most often fail at the TOOTH rather than in the porcelain, through decay at the edge or a fracture in the root, which is why that edge is where cleaning matters most.

Can a capped tooth still decay?

Yes, at the line where the restoration meets the tooth. The ceramic itself cannot decay; the tooth underneath it can. Floss or use an interdental brush at that line every day, and keep your recall appointments so the edge is checked while a problem is still small.

Do I need root canal treatment first?

Not usually. The nerve is treated when it is already infected or irreversibly inflamed, not because a restoration is planned on top of it. If your tooth needs both, the nerve is dealt with first and the cap follows it.

Can the shade be lightened later?

No. Zirconia and porcelain do not respond to whitening agents. If you intend to lighten your natural teeth, do that first, let the shade settle for about two weeks, then have the restoration matched to the result. The order matters and it cannot be reversed afterwards.

Do I need a night guard?

If you grind or clench, yes, and we will recommend one. Grinding is the most common cause of a fractured unit, and a guard is a far smaller undertaking than a remake.

Which clinic can I book?

We have four clinics in Metro Manila: Mitsukoshi BGC, Shangri-La Plaza, Bonifacio Stopover BGC and Alabang Town Center. Which of them schedule this treatment, and on which days, is still being confirmed with the practice, so book through the appointment control and you will be placed at the branch that runs your case.

The first week

  • Chew on the other side for the first day, until you have learned where the new surface sits.
  • Brush normally, including along the gum line, from the day it is fitted.
  • Floss by pulling the thread out sideways rather than snapping it back up past a new contact.
  • If you grind at night, wear the guard every night, not on the bad nights only.
  • Bring any previous radiographs, and a note of any tooth that is currently sore, to the first appointment.

A first appointment gives you three things: an examination against what is actually wrong, a plan naming the material and the sequence, and a written quote. None of them commits you to treatment.
