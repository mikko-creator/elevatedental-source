Stage 01, plain English

What a zirconia veneer is

A zirconia veneer is a shell of zirconium dioxide, milled from a solid ceramic block and bonded to the prepared front surface of a tooth.

Of the two ceramics offered for veneer work here it is the denser and more fracture resistant, and it is the more opaque. Opacity is not a flaw. It is the reason this material gets specified at all.

A tooth gone gray after root canal treatment Tetracycline banding A dark metal core underneath

Those three show through a translucent ceramic. Zirconia covers them. That single property is what the rest of this page is organized around.

It also does not take up pigment the way a directly bonded resin does. Coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco mark the polished ceramic surface far less readily than they mark composite, which is the second reason it gets specified.

The honest part

This treatment is irreversible. A layer of enamel is removed to make room for the shell, usually between half a millimeter and a millimeter on the facing surface. Once that enamel is gone the tooth is committed to being restored for the rest of its life. You will be told this at the consultation, not in the small print, and it is the first thing discussed rather than the last.

What it is for

The problem this treatment solves is a specific one: front teeth that are discolored, chipped, worn, slightly rotated or unevenly sized, where the discoloration is deep rather than sitting on the surface, and where the correction has to hold up to a strong bite.

Stage 02, the real choice

Zirconia or Emax

There are two hard ceramic options. The other is lithium disilicate, sold as Emax, and it is used on this practice's porcelain veneer cases.

Both are on the same menu here, which is why the recommendation can be made on the merits of your teeth rather than on what the clinic happens to offer.

The material is chosen by what your case needs hidden, or shown.

Five properties, two materials, no winner declared in the markup
PropertyZirconiaEmax lithium disilicate
Opacity More opaque. Covers a gray, banded or metal-cored tooth underneath. More translucent. A dark substrate can read through it.
Fracture resistance The denser and more fracture resistant of the two. Strong, and the more usual choice where load is moderate.
Stain behavior The glazed surface resists staining well. Also holds its shade. Neither takes up pigment the way composite does.
Repair Does not repair the way composite does. A chipped unit is normally replaced. Also a remake rather than a chairside patch once fractured.
Natural light behavior Reads more solid. The property that hides a dark tooth also flattens the edge. Usually reads more naturally on an upper central incisor where the underlying color is already good.

Read the last row twice

If your priority is the light scattering translucency of a natural upper central incisor and the tooth underneath is already a good color, Emax will usually read better than zirconia. That is the honest answer even on the zirconia page, and it is the answer you will be given at the chair.

Stage 03

Whether zirconia fits your case, and where the route forks

Zirconia suits deep intrinsic discoloration that whitening cannot lift, worn or chipped edges on front teeth, small gaps or minor rotations you do not want to spend two years correcting, and the patient who grinds and has already had a more translucent ceramic fail.

They are not for everyone. If one of the four turnings below describes you, take it, and the treatment named on it is the one to read next.

Turning 01 Move first

Straighten first, then veneer

If your teeth are significantly out of alignment, veneers are the wrong instrument. Moving teeth is orthodontics. Covering them is cosmetics. Ten ceramic units used to disguise a bite problem is a bigger and less stable answer than braces or Invisalign (Clear Aligners) followed by two or three veneers, and the orthodontists here will say so before the cosmetic dentists prepare anything.

Braces Clear aligners
Turning 02 Protect first

A nightguard, or no ceramic

If you grind heavily and will not wear a nightguard, no ceramic option is a sound investment. Zirconia is strong. It is not indestructible, and neither is the tooth carrying it.

General dentistry
Turning 03 Full support

A crown, not a veneer

If the tooth has lost most of its structure, a veneer does not have enough left to bond to. A crown is the correct restoration in that case, and you will be told so rather than sold the more cosmetic sounding option.

Dental crowns
Turning 04 Other ceramic

Emax, for translucency

If what you want is the light behavior of a natural incisor and your underlying tooth color is already good, the more translucent ceramic is the better instrument. This is the same trade set out in stage 02, arriving here as a decision rather than a table.

Porcelain veneers

Before any of the four

Active decay and gum disease are treated first. A veneer bonded over an unstable foundation is a wasted veneer, and your assessment will say so plainly. That is a precondition of every path on this page, not a fifth alternative to weigh against the others.

Stage 04, consultation to review

The seven appointments

Seven steps, in this order, with the real time each one takes.

Step 03 matters more than the other six. It is the only point at which you can still change your mind for nothing, because it happens before a single surface has been cut and before the laboratory has been given anything to build. Everything after it costs either tooth structure or a remake, which is why it is a separate visit rather than five minutes bolted onto another one.

The seven appointments
StepWhat happensHow long
01 Consultation and records Examination, photographs, a digital scan of your teeth, and radiographs where they are needed to check the health of the teeth involved. Your shade, your bite and your gum line are all recorded. You are given the fee for your case in writing at this stage. 60 to 90 minutes
02 Smile design Your case is designed against the proportions of your face, not against a chart. You see the proposed shape and length before anything is cut. 1 to 2 weeks
03 Trial smile The design is transferred into your mouth in temporary material so you can look at it, speak with it and take it home to think about. This is where changes are still free. 45 to 60 minutes
04 Preparation and temporaries Under local anesthesia the facing surfaces are prepared and a final scan is taken. You leave with temporary veneers that match the approved design. 2 to 3 hours
05 Laboratory fabrication The units are milled, characterized and glazed. 1 to 2 weeks
06 Fitting and bonding The veneers are tried in, checked against your face and your bite, then bonded. Your bite is adjusted at the same visit. 1 to 2 hours
07 Review The gums are checked as they settle around the new margins, and the bite is confirmed a second time once you are chewing normally again. 20 minutes, 1 to 2 weeks later

Stage 05, the bench

Who performs it

The work is carried out by a cosmetic dentistry specialist. The design is theirs, the preparation is theirs, and the bonding is theirs.

If the assessment finds a bite problem, gum recession or a compromised root under one of the teeth you want veneered, the clinician who deals with it works in the same practice. Your case does not get handed to a clinic across town and then handed back.

Cosmetic Dentistry

Owns the case. Shade, shape, proportion, the preparation depth and the bonding are decided and carried out here.

Orthodontics

Where a bite problem is found, the movement is planned here first and the ceramic follows the alignment rather than disguising it.

Periodontics

Where there is gum recession or inflamed tissue, the gum is treated before any margin is placed against it.

Endodontics

Where a root under one of the teeth you want veneered is compromised, it is dealt with here before the tooth is committed to a restoration.

The three systems that carry a case

Three named instruments carry a veneer case here.

We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

Station 01

RayFace facial scanner

A three dimensional scan of your face. Tooth width, midline and incisal edge position are set against your lip line and your facial midline rather than against a generic template, which is what stops a technically correct set of teeth from looking wrong on the person wearing them.

Named installed system
Station 02

iTero intraoral scanner

Your preparations and your opposing teeth are captured digitally instead of in impression putty. The scan goes to the laboratory as data, so the fit is checked before the ceramic is milled rather than after.

Named installed system
Station 03

OccluSense bite analysis

Where your bite is a factor, and with veneers on worn or ground down teeth it usually is, this records where and how hard your teeth meet. Contact points that would load a new ceramic edge unevenly are found and adjusted at the fitting appointment.

Named installed system

The sequencing rule

Zoom whitening is available here, and it matters to the ORDER of your treatment rather than to the veneers themselves. Ceramic does not respond to bleach. If you intend to whiten the natural teeth you are keeping, that is done first and allowed to settle, and only then is the veneer shade chosen, so the ceramic is matched to the color you are going to live with.

What it costs

From ₱38,000 A representative market figure, not an Elevate quote

Per tooth. Anchored to published market ranges, not to an Elevate price list.

Every peso amount on this page is representative and anchored to published market ranges - it is not a price quoted by Elevate Dental. Elevate quotes your own figure per treatment, after a clinical assessment.

What moves the final figure

  • Case complexity. A single veneer replacing a chipped edge is not the same job as a ten unit upper design that has to re-establish your midline and incisal plane.
  • Material and finish. Monolithic zirconia, layered zirconia and a hand characterized anterior unit sit at different laboratory costs.
  • Number of units. The design work is shared across the case, so the per-tooth figure behaves differently for two teeth than for eight. The number that matters to you is the total for your case, and that is the one you are given.

The exact figure is confirmed at consultation, after your teeth have been examined and scanned, and it is given to you in writing before any preparation is booked.

Your own figure comes from your own teeth, so the only way to get it is an examination and a written quote.

Stage 08, the end of the route

Questions, aftercare, and what a first appointment gives you

Four questions people ask before they agree to anything, and four they ask once the ceramic is in.

Before a tooth is prepared

Will they look fake?

That is a design question, not a material question. The shade, the length, the edge translucency and the width to height ratio are all decided at the trial smile stage, in your mouth, before anything is milled. If it looks wrong at that appointment, it gets changed at that appointment.

Does it hurt?

Preparation is done under local anesthesia and you should not feel the work itself. Expect some sensitivity to cold and to pressure for a few days between preparation and fitting, while you are in temporaries. Ordinary analgesia handles it. Tell us if it does not.

What if I do not like the result?

This is what step 03 in stage 04 exists to prevent. You sign off on the shape while it is still temporary material in your mouth, and nothing is cut until you do. After that point a change means new units at cost, so the appointment is worth taking seriously rather than treating as a formality.

Which clinic can I book?

There are four clinics in Metro Manila: Mitsukoshi BGC, Shangri-La Plaza, Bonifacio Stopover BGC and Alabang Town Center. Which of them schedule this treatment, and on which days, is being confirmed with the practice, so book through the appointment control and you will be placed at the branch that runs your case.

Once the ceramic is in

How long do zirconia veneers last?

Ceramic veneers are commonly quoted with a service life in the region of ten to fifteen years. That is a general clinical range and not a commitment made about your case. Yours will depend on three things you control: whether you grind, whether you wear a nightguard if you do, and how well you keep the margins clean. Plan for eventual replacement rather than treating them as a one time purchase.

Can zirconia veneers stain?

The glazed ceramic surface resists staining well. The bonding margin at the gum line is a different surface and can pick up stain if it is not cleaned properly. This is one of the reasons the six month recall matters.

Do I still need to floss?

Yes, and more carefully than before. The veneer does not decay. The tooth underneath it does, and the margin where the two meet is exactly where decay starts. Floss the margins daily.

Can a unit be repaired if it chips?

Zirconia does not repair the way composite does. A chipped unit is normally replaced. That is a reason to take the nightguard advice seriously. Replacement and remake terms are pending the practice's written policy and are not stated on this page until they are supplied.

Warranty and replacement terms are not stated on this page.

Aftercare

  • Wear your nightguard every night if you have been given one.
  • Brush twice daily and floss the margins once daily.
  • Do not use your front teeth to open packaging, bite nails or crack ice.
  • Keep the six month recall so the margins and the bite can be checked while a problem is still small.
  • If a veneer feels high when you bite, call the clinic rather than waiting for the next recall.
