What this field covers

Why a pocket is the whole problem

Your teeth are held by fibers anchored into bone. Bacteria collect at the gum margin and harden into calculus, and the body's response to that deposit breaks down the attachment. The gum separates from the tooth and a pocket forms.

Once a pocket is deep enough, a toothbrush cannot reach the bottom of it, and no amount of brushing at home will.

  • Bleeding. Gums that bleed when you brush or floss.
  • Recession. Gums that have receded and left the root exposed.
  • Depth. Pockets between gum and tooth that a normal clean cannot reach.
  • Breath. Persistent bad breath coming from bacteria in the pocket itself.
  • Movement. Teeth that have started to loosen or drift.
  • Contour. An uneven or heavy gum line, where contouring is indicated.
  • Around an implant. The gum and bone surrounding one already placed.
Why this field sits underneath

Most other dental work is built on top of this one.

The one route Below the margin

Scaling and Root Planing

Deep cleaning below the gumline. Hardened deposits are removed from the root surface and the root is smoothed, so the tissue has a clean surface to reattach to. This is the treatment for pockets that a routine cleaning cannot reach. It is worked through in sections, with local anesthesia where the depth calls for it, and the pockets are re-measured afterwards to check the response.

The honest half

The difference the timing makes

The same condition, met at two different moments. What changes between them is not the treatment, it is what the treatment can still achieve.

Met early Controlled

At the bleeding stage

The attachment is inflamed but the supporting bone is largely intact, and cleaning under the margin plus a maintenance interval is what controls it. The work is smaller, the appointments are fewer, and what is being protected is still there.

Met late Managed

After bone has gone

Left alone the condition takes bone, and the bone does not come back by itself. Treatment then aims at stopping further loss and keeping what remains, which is a different goal from the one above and is described as such rather than dressed up.

Why a hub page says this out loud

Being told plainly that one stage is controllable and the other is manageable is not a scare tactic and it is not an upsell. It is the reason an appointment for something that does not hurt is worth making, and you are better served reading it here than hearing it after a chart has been filled in.

The specialists in this field

What is built on top of this

Gum disease is diagnosed by measurement, not by appearance. A periodontist charts the depth of every pocket around every tooth, reads bone level against the imaging, and decides from those numbers what the tissue can recover and what it cannot. Gums can look healthy over a pocket that is losing bone.

If a crown is planned

Prosthodontics

Crowns, bridges and dentures are measured to a margin that has to stay where it is. That is why the foundation is assessed before the restoration is designed rather than after.

If an implant is planned

Oral Surgery

An implant is anchored in bone, so how much bone there is and how healthy the tissue around it is are the first questions asked, and they are asked again at every review afterwards.

Orthodontics

Moving a tooth means moving it through bone. Treatment that begins on an untreated foundation is planned on ground that is still changing, so the order matters.

Technology used here

Measurement first, then instrumentation

Periodontal treatment is measured before it is worked, and the instruments are chosen for the stage the tissue is at.

01

3D CBCT imaging

Shows bone level around each root in three dimensions, so the extent of the loss is measured rather than estimated from a flat film. Available at all four clinics.

02

Sol Dental Laser

Used for gum contouring where it is indicated, and for soft-tissue work that would otherwise call for a scalpel and sutures.

03

AirFlow (guided biofilm therapy)

Lifts biofilm and surface staining with a controlled jet of air, water and fine powder. Used at maintenance appointments while the tissue is still settling and instrumentation would be heavy-handed.
