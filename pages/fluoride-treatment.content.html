Stage 01, the mechanism

Decay is chemistry before it is a hole

Bacteria in plaque turn sugar into acid, the acid pulls mineral out of the enamel surface, and if that runs faster than saliva can put mineral back, the surface eventually collapses into a cavity.

Fluoride changes that chemistry. Present at the enamel surface, it is incorporated into the crystal structure as fluorapatite, which dissolves at a lower pH than the mineral it replaces. In plain terms the enamel becomes harder to dissolve.

It also slows the bacteria's acid production, and it helps drive mineral back into an early lesion.

Which is why timing matters

A white spot caught early can remineralize rather than progress. That is the entire window this treatment works in, and it is why the examination that finds the spot is worth more than the varnish that treats it. Once decay has broken through into the dentine the tissue is gone, and no application brings it back.

Stage 02, the number

Why not just the toothpaste at home

22,600 Parts per million in a 5 percent sodium fluoride varnish

Against roughly 1,000 to 1,500 in a family toothpaste. General clinical values for the material class, not Elevate measurements.

What is used in clinic is a different thing from toothpaste.

Toothpaste is a low concentration used twice a day. A varnish applied in clinic is a high concentration held against the tooth for hours. The varnish sets on contact with saliva and stays where it is put while the tooth takes up fluoride, which is the whole reason the instruction afterwards is to leave it alone until the next morning.

Gel and foam applied in a tray are the alternative delivery. Varnish is generally preferred in young children because a smaller volume is used and less is swallowed.

It works alongside the tube at home. It does not replace it.

Stage 03

Who gains most, and who does not

This is worth most where decay risk is genuinely elevated, and establishing that is what your first appointment does.

It suits a child who has already had decay, because past decay is the strongest predictor of future decay. It suits a child with deep grooves, crowded teeth or enamel defects. It suits anyone in a fixed appliance, because brackets hold plaque against the enamel for the length of treatment and the white marks that leaves are permanent.

It suits patients with a dry mouth, whether from medication, a medical condition or radiotherapy, because saliva is the body's own remineralizing system and without it the balance shifts sharply. It suits adults with exposed root surfaces from gum recession, since root surface is softer than enamel and decays faster, and it helps with the sensitivity that goes with those roots.

The four honest limits

  1. It does not repair a cavity. Where decay has already broken through enamel into the dentine the tissue is gone, and no amount of varnish brings it back. That tooth needs restoring. This works on the surface and on early lesions, not on established holes.
  2. It does not replace brushing, flossing or diet. A child who snacks on sweet drinks all afternoon will out-produce any varnish. If your consultation identifies sugar frequency as the real problem, you will be told that plainly, because buying repeat applications while nothing else changes is money spent to no purpose.
  3. Total intake has to be counted, not guessed. Very young children swallow some of what is applied, and excess during the years the permanent teeth are forming can cause fluorosis, a cosmetic mottling of enamel. Professional application is dose-controlled and applied in small quantities for exactly this reason. Tell us what your child already gets, including toothpaste strength, any mouthrinse and any supplement.
  4. Tell us about a resin allergy first. If your child has a known allergy to colophony or any resin, say so before the appointment, because some varnishes contain it and an alternative is used.

Stage 04, the appointment

Seven steps, and one instruction for the evening

This is short. It is normally done at the end of a check-up or a cleaning appointment and adds about 5 to 10 minutes to the visit.

The instruction at step 06 is the one that decides how much benefit you actually get. The longer the coating stays in contact, the more fluoride the tooth takes up, so the evening after the appointment is part of the treatment rather than after it.

Seven steps, and what each one takes
StepWhat happensHow long
01 Assessment Decay history, diet, brushing, medical history, existing exposure and any early white-spot lesions are recorded. This produces a decay risk level, and that level decides the interval rather than a standard schedule applied to everyone. Part of the examination
02 Cleaning, if it is included This is applied to a clean surface, so where a professional cleaning is part of the same visit it comes first. 10 to 30 minutes
03 Drying and isolation The teeth are dried with air and cotton rolls, because the coating adheres better to a dry surface. 1 to 2 minutes
04 Application It is painted onto all surfaces with a small brush, including between the teeth where the applicator can reach. There is no injection and nothing that has to be held in the mouth. A gel or foam in a tray, if that is used instead, is held for about four minutes. 2 to 3 minutes
05 Setting It hardens on contact with saliva. The teeth may look slightly dull or yellow for a few hours: that is the coating, not the tooth, and it brushes off the next day. Seconds
06 Instructions No hot drinks, no crunchy or sticky food, and no brushing until the following morning. The rest of that day
07 Recall The interval is reviewed each time rather than left running indefinitely, so a level set during a difficult year can come back down. Every 3 to 6 months

Stage 05, judgement over equipment

The part that is not the varnish

For children this is delivered by a pediatric dentistry specialist, and the application itself takes minutes.

The part that matters is the assessment in front of it.

That assessment decides whether the interval is six months or three, and what else has to change for either to be worth doing. A brush and a dose of varnish is not a service. A correct interval is.

If the examination finds decay that needs restoring, grooves that would be better sealed, or crowding an orthodontist should see early, those clinicians work in the same practice and can see your child without you starting the search again elsewhere.

What the named systems do here, and do not

  • A brush and a dose. There is no machine involved in the application, and this page will not attach one to it in order to sound more advanced.
  • AirFlow (guided biofilm therapy). Operated at our clinics for professional cleaning. This is applied to a clean tooth surface, so where a cleaning appointment is part of the same visit, that is the system which may be used for it.
  • Digital dental radiography. Early decay between the back teeth is invisible to the eye. A radiograph, taken only where indicated, is what distinguishes a lesion that can still be arrested from one that has passed the point where restoring it is required. That distinction is the difference between the correct treatment and a wasted one.
  • 3D CBCT imaging. Available at all four clinics, and it has NO role in this appointment. Your child will not be scanned for one. In pediatric dentistry it answers a different question, such as locating an unerupted or impacted permanent tooth.

What it costs

This starts at ₱1,200, a representative market figure rather than a quoted Elevate fee, and what you are actually buying is an interval rather than a single appointment.

How you pay

Elevate Dental does not accept HMO or dental insurance. All treatment is self-pay, and HMO coverage cannot be used for dental treatments at our clinics. Payment is by cash, bank cheque, bank transfer, debit or credit card, or QR payment.

What moves the final figure

  1. What the visit includes. Applied at the end of an examination and cleaning is not the same appointment as applied alone, and the fee reflects which one you are booking.
  2. Material. Varnish, gel and foam sit at slightly different costs, and the choice is clinical rather than commercial. Varnish is generally used for young children.
  3. Frequency. A high-risk patient on a short interval is buying four applications a year rather than two. That interval is set at the examination and reviewed at each recall, so it can come down again.

The exact figure is confirmed at consultation and given to you in writing before treatment starts.

Questions, and the evening afterwards

In the doses used professionally it is applied in small, measured quantities specifically to limit how much is swallowed. The genuine concern is TOTAL intake during the years permanent teeth are forming, which is why we ask what your child already uses at home rather than assuming. If you would prefer not to have it applied, say so, and the alternative preventive measures will be set out for you honestly, including what they do and do not achieve.

Before you book

How often does my child need it?

Commonly twice a year, alongside the check-up, and four times where decay risk is high, which usually means recent decay, a fixed appliance, a dry mouth or enamel defects. The interval comes from the assessment and is reviewed at each visit, so it is not a permanent commitment.

Does it hurt?

No injection and no instrument on the tooth. Your child will feel the applicator and will taste the coating. The most common complaint is the texture on the teeth for a few hours afterwards.

Can it reverse a cavity?

It can arrest and remineralize an early lesion that has not yet broken through the surface. It cannot rebuild tissue that has already been lost. The radiograph and the examination are what tell you which of the two you are dealing with.

Do adults need it?

Some do. Exposed root surfaces from gum recession, a dry mouth from medication, orthodontic treatment, or a run of new decay in adulthood are all reasons for professional application. It is not a childhood-only measure.

That evening, and the next morning

What can my child eat afterwards?

Soft, cool food for the next few hours. Avoid hot drinks, crunchy food and anything sticky. Do not brush until the following morning, so the coating stays in contact overnight. Normal eating resumes the next day.

Will their teeth look yellow?

There is a dull or slightly yellow film for a few hours. It is the coating, not a stain on the tooth, and it comes away with the first brushing the next morning.

Is this the same as what is in toothpaste?

No, and stage 02 gives the numbers. The concentration in a professional varnish is many times higher and it stays on the tooth for hours rather than seconds. It works alongside daily fluoride toothpaste rather than replacing it.

What if we miss the recall?

Rebook it. The interval was set from a risk level, and a level set during a difficult year is reviewed each visit rather than left running, so a missed appointment is worth rescheduling rather than abandoning.

Aftercare

  • No hot drinks and no crunchy or sticky food for the rest of the day.
  • No brushing or flossing until the next morning.
  • From the following day, brush twice daily with a fluoride toothpaste, and spit rather than rinse so some fluoride remains.
  • Floss between any teeth that touch.
  • Reduce how OFTEN sugar is eaten rather than only how much, because frequency is what drives the acid cycle.
  • Keep the recall interval you have been given.
