Stage 01, plain English

What whitening actually changes

Tooth colour has two components, and only one of them is reachable by cleaning.

Marking on the surface sits on top of the enamel and lifts off at a routine cleaning appointment. Intrinsic colour is inside the tooth, in the dentine beneath the enamel, and no amount of cleaning reaches it. This treatment works on the second one.

The gel is a peroxide compound. It penetrates the enamel and breaks the larger pigmented molecules held in the dentine into smaller ones that reflect more light, so the tooth reads lighter. Nothing is removed from the tooth and no layer is added to it. The structure is unchanged. The colour of what is already there is altered.

Zoom is a light-activated system. A controlled light source is directed at the gel to accelerate the reaction across all the treated teeth at once, in measured intervals, with your gums, lips and cheeks isolated behind a protective barrier the entire time.

Why it is done here rather than at home

In-clinic whitening is supervised whitening. The gel is stronger than anything sold over a counter, which is exactly why it is applied by a clinician, behind a barrier, on a mouth that has been examined first.

Take-home trays fitted to your own impressions are a legitimate option, and a slower one. Generic strips and counter kits use lower concentrations, are not shaped to your teeth, and can irritate the gum where the gel spreads.

What it cannot reach

Deep intrinsic discolouration, such as tetracycline banding, responds poorly and sometimes unevenly. A single dark non-vital tooth is a different problem with a different treatment, usually internal whitening of that tooth alone. Fluorosis, white spot lesions and dark cracks each behave differently under gel, and are assessed individually rather than promised a shade.

Stage 02

Who it suits, and what it will not touch

This suits you if your teeth are healthy and your gums are healthy, and your natural shade has yellowed with age, coffee, tea, red wine, soy or tobacco.

Yellow and orange-brown shades generally respond better than grey ones. It also suits patients preparing for a specific event, provided there is time in the calendar for the clean beforehand and for sensitivity to settle.

Existing restorations

Gel does not change the colour of any material that is not tooth. Crowns, veneers, bridges, composite fillings and bonded edges stay exactly the shade they were made. If you have a restoration on a front tooth, treating the teeth around it will make that restoration stand out, and it will need replacing to match afterwards.

We will show you this on your own teeth before you decide, not afterwards.

Where it sits in a plan

If you intend to have cosmetic work later, whitening first and then matching the restorations to the new shade is the correct sequence. Doing it the other way round means paying twice for the same match. Where this is one stage of a larger plan, the order is set with the prosthodontics and orthodontics specialists in the same group.

Where we say no

Untreated decay, leaking restorations, exposed dentine and active gum disease are treated before any gel is dispensed, not through it. Peroxide reaching an unsealed cavity or inflamed tissue causes real pain. We do not treat during pregnancy or while nursing, and we do not treat patients under 18 without a specific clinical assessment.

If sensitivity is your main concern, say so at the consultation. It changes how we plan the session rather than whether you can have one.

Stage 03, the session

How the appointment runs

Allow about 90 minutes to two hours in the clinic for the session itself, plus the earlier consultation and clean.

Step 01 20 to 30 min

Consultation and examination

Decay check, gum check, and a review of every existing restoration on a visible tooth. The shade you begin at is logged against the rack and photographed, so the change is measured rather than remembered.

Step 02 Separate visit

Professional clean beforehand

This is carried out on clean teeth. Surface deposit blocks the gel unevenly, and an uneven start produces an uneven finish. Most patients have oral prophylaxis first.

Step 03 15 to 20 min

Isolation and protection

A retractor holds the lips and cheeks clear, a barrier is placed and cured along the gum line, and the soft tissue is sealed off before anything is dispensed. This step is most of the reason the concentration is safe in a clinic.

Step 04 45 to 60 min

Gel and light cycles

The gel is applied to the front teeth and activated in timed cycles, typically three or four of about 15 minutes each, with fresh gel between cycles. You rest through it.

Step 05 15 to 20 min

Removal and final reading

The barrier is removed, a desensitizing agent is applied, and the finishing shade is read against the same guide and photographed alongside the first reading.

Step 06 About 10 min

Aftercare briefing

The two days that follow matter more than the rest of the month, and you leave knowing exactly why. Stage 06 carries the same instructions in writing.

What moves the final figure

  • How many teeth are treated, and whether both arches are included.
  • Whether a professional clean is needed first, which is priced separately.
  • Whether take-home trays are supplied alongside the session for maintenance.
  • How many light cycles your case needs.
  • Whether existing restorations on visible teeth will need replacing afterwards to match, which is quoted as its own treatment.

The same guide is read at both ends of the session and photographed under the same conditions, so what changed is documented rather than described. How much lighter your teeth go depends on the type and depth of your discolouration, your enamel thickness and where you begin. We will not put a number of shades on your result before we have examined your teeth, and no clinic honestly can.

The change relapses gradually. Teeth take up colour from what passes over them every day, so coffee, tea, red wine, dark sauces and tobacco will move it back toward where it started. How fast depends entirely on your habits. Many patients maintain with take-home trays or a repeat session at intervals, planned with their clinician.

Stage 06, the end of the route

Questions, and the window afterwards

For the first two days the enamel is at its most receptive to pigment, so what you eat then matters more than what you eat for the rest of the month.

  • Keep to pale food and drink: water, milk, plain rice, chicken, white fish, potato.
  • Avoid coffee, tea, cola, red wine, soy sauce, curry, beetroot, berries and tobacco entirely in that window.
  • Use a desensitizing toothpaste if cold bothers you.
  • Rinse with water after coffee or wine from then on, and keep your recall appointments so deposit is removed before it sets.

How white will my teeth get?

That depends on your starting point and the type of discolouration, and it is assessed on your own teeth at the consultation. Yellow tones generally lift more readily than grey ones. Anyone quoting you a specific number of shades before looking in your mouth is guessing.

How long does it last?

It fades gradually rather than ending on a date. With careful habits and maintenance the change holds considerably longer than with none.

Does it damage enamel?

The gel works by altering pigment inside the tooth, not by removing tooth structure. It is used at clinical concentration, behind a gum barrier, on a mouth that has been examined first, and that supervision is the difference between whitening and harm.

Will my crowns, veneers and fillings change too?

No. Restorative materials do not respond to the gel. If you have visible restorations, treating the natural teeth around them will change how well they match, and they may need replacing afterwards. This is discussed and priced before you start, not after.

Is it uncomfortable?

Cold sensitivity and occasional short zings during and after the session are common, and a desensitizing agent is applied at the end. Sensitivity in the 24 to 48 hours afterwards is expected and typically settles on its own. Tell us at the consultation if your teeth are already tender, and the protocol is adjusted for you.

Can I do it at home instead?

Trays fitted to your own impressions are a legitimate route and a slower one. Counter kits are not shaped to your teeth and can irritate the gum where the gel spreads. We will tell you which suits your case rather than which we would rather sell.
