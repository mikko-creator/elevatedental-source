What this field covers

The diagnostic and preventive layer

Your dentist examines the teeth, gums and bite. Images are taken where an examination alone cannot answer the question. Deposits are removed. Teeth that have started to break down are restored before the damage reaches the nerve.

  • Deposited on the tooth. Plaque and tartar on the crown and under the gum edge.
  • Deposited on the tooth. Surface staining from coffee, tea and tobacco.
  • Happening to the tooth. Decay and cavities.
  • Happening to the tooth. Chipped, worn or cracked edges.
  • Happening to the tooth. Sensitivity to hot, cold and sweet.
  • Happening to the gum. Early inflammation and bleeding.
  • Watched over time. Teeth already filled, crowned or root-treated.

Which appointment is yours

Cleaning or repair

One question separates them, and you can usually answer it yourself before you book.

Nothing is broken Cleaning

Book a cleaning when the surfaces are sound

The teeth are intact, nothing is sensitive to bite on, and the complaint is roughness, staining or a gum that bleeds when you brush. That is a deposit problem. Your dentist decides between the two cleaning routes on what has actually built up and how deep into the pocket it reaches.

Something is broken Repair

Book a repair when structure has been lost

There is a visible hole, a chipped edge, a rough patch your tongue keeps finding, or a tooth that reacts to cold and settles again. That is a structure problem, and caught at this stage it is usually one appointment. Left alone it becomes the appointment two fields further down this page.

If you cannot tell

Book the check-up and let the examination decide. A cleaning is often added to a repair visit anyway, and the sequence is agreed with you before either one starts.

The honest limit

What a check-up finds that this field does not treat

These four findings are common at a routine visit. Each belongs to a different specialist, and each of those specialists works in this building.

Decay has reached the nerve

Endodontics

Once the pulp is involved a filling no longer settles it. Root canal treatment saves the tooth, and the case moves across without you retelling it.

Endodontics
The bleeding has reached the bone

Periodontics

Gum inflammation that has begun to cost bone is past what a routine clean can hold. Deep cleaning under the gum is a different appointment with a different specialist.

Periodontics
An old crown is failing

Prosthodontics

When too little sound structure is left for composite to bond to, the answer is a crown, a bridge or a denture rather than a larger filling.

Prosthodontics
The tooth cannot be kept

Oral Surgery

A cracked root or an impacted wisdom tooth is a surgical problem. It is planned on a scan first, and what replaces the space is planned at the same time.
