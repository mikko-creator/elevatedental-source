## §2 - What it is

Brushing and flossing control plaque. They do not remove tartar. Once plaque mineralizes against the tooth it becomes calculus, a hard deposit bonded to enamel and to the root surface below the gum line, and no home routine will shift it. Calculus holds bacteria against the gum, and that is what turns healthy tissue into bleeding, receding tissue.

Oral prophylaxis is the appointment that removes it. An ultrasonic scaler breaks the deposit off the tooth with high-frequency vibration and a water spray, hand instruments finish the areas the tip cannot reach, and the surfaces are then polished so they are smooth rather than microscopically rough. Smooth surfaces give new plaque less to hold on to.

The examination is part of the treatment, not an add-on to it. In the same visit your dentist charts gum condition, checks for decay, looks at the soft tissue, and tells you what is changing since your last visit. Most decay and most gum disease are found at a routine clean, while they are still small problems.

This is the standard maintenance appointment for adults and for children with their permanent teeth.

## §3 - Who it suits

Oral prophylaxis suits nearly everyone, on a schedule. It is the correct treatment if your gums are healthy or mildly inflamed, if you have visible tartar behind the lower front teeth or along the gum line, and if you simply want the six-month maintenance visit done properly.

It suits you particularly if it has been a while. A long gap does not disqualify you from a routine clean; it usually just means a longer appointment, and occasionally a second one.

**Where it is not the right treatment.**

If pockets have formed between tooth and gum, if the gum bleeds heavily and generally, or if bone loss shows on imaging, a surface clean is not enough and is not what we will do. Deposits under the gum have to be removed from the root surface itself. That treatment is tooth scaling and root planing, it is performed by our periodontics specialists, and it usually runs over more than one visit. We will say so at the assessment rather than clean the surface and send you home with the real problem untouched.

If you have untreated decay, the cavity is restored separately. A clean does not repair a cavity.

If your concern is the color of your teeth rather than the health of your gums, prophylaxis will remove surface stain but will not lighten your natural shade. Teeth whitening is the treatment for that, and it is only carried out on a mouth that is already clean and healthy.

Tell us beforehand if you are pregnant, take a blood thinner, have a heart valve or joint replacement, or have any condition that changes how we handle bleeding or infection risk.

## §4 - How it works

1. **Consultation and examination (10 to 15 minutes).** Medical history, soft-tissue check, decay check, and a gum chart that records pocket depths and bleeding points. This is the step that decides whether a routine clean is the right treatment at all.
2. **Imaging if indicated (5 to 10 minutes).** Taken only where the examination raises a question, such as suspected decay between teeth or suspected bone loss.
3. **Ultrasonic scaling (10 to 20 minutes).** Calculus is removed from the crown surfaces and the gum margin with an ultrasonic tip and water spray, quadrant by quadrant.
4. **Hand instrumentation (5 to 10 minutes).** The areas an ultrasonic tip cannot finish, particularly tight contacts between the front teeth, are cleared by hand.
5. **Polishing (about 5 minutes).** Surfaces are polished smooth. Where surface stain is heavy, AirFlow (guided biofilm therapy) may be used instead of a paste.
6. **Fluoride and review (about 5 minutes).** Fluoride is applied where indicated, and you are shown the specific areas your home routine is missing.

A routine maintenance appointment runs 45 minutes to an hour. A first visit, or a visit after several years, runs longer and is occasionally split across two appointments so that no quadrant is rushed.

## §6 - The technology behind it

**Ultrasonic scaling.** The working instrument for calculus removal, with water irrigation to keep the tooth cool and flush the deposit clear.

**AirFlow (guided biofilm therapy).** Used in place of a polishing paste where stain and biofilm are heavy, around brackets and implants, and on exposed root surfaces where an abrasive paste would be uncomfortable.

**3D CBCT imaging.** Installed at all four of our clinics. Where the gum chart suggests bone loss, the periodontal picture is measured in three dimensions in the same building rather than estimated from a flat image or referred out for a scan.

## §8 - Results

After a routine clean the tooth surfaces are smooth, the gum margin is free of the deposit that was irritating it, and light surface stain is gone. Gums that bled on brushing usually stop within one to two weeks, provided the home routine keeps the plaque down. Some patients notice slight sensitivity to cold for a few days where tartar had been covering an exposed root surface; it settles.

What a clean does not do is change your natural tooth shade, close a gap, or repair a cavity.

## §9 - FAQ and closing CTA

**How often do I need this?**
Every six months for most adults. Your dentist may set a shorter interval if you smoke, wear fixed braces, have implants, are pregnant, have diabetes, or have a history of gum disease. The interval is set from your gum chart, not from a default.

**Does it hurt?**
Most patients feel vibration, water and light pressure rather than pain. Sensitivity is more common where the gum has receded and the root surface is exposed. Tell your dentist during the appointment and the technique, the tip and the water temperature are adjusted. Where an area is genuinely tender, local anesthetic is available.

**Why do my gums bleed during the clean?**
Because they are inflamed. Healthy gums do not usually bleed under an instrument. Bleeding is the sign that the deposit needed removing, and it typically stops within one to two weeks afterwards.

**Will scaling loosen my teeth or damage the enamel?**
No. Calculus can be splinting teeth that are already mobile from bone loss, and removing it can make that pre-existing mobility noticeable. The scaler works on the deposit, not the enamel. If your teeth are mobile, that is a periodontal finding and it is charted and treated, not ignored.

**Will it whiten my teeth?**
It removes surface stain and returns the tooth to its own natural shade. It does not lighten the natural shade. That is teeth whitening, and it is a separate treatment carried out after the mouth is clean and healthy.

**What is the difference between this and scaling and root planing?**
Oral prophylaxis treats the crown surfaces and the gum margin in a healthy or mildly inflamed mouth. Scaling and root planing treats the root surface inside a periodontal pocket, is performed under local anesthetic, and is usually split over more than one visit. Your gum chart decides which one you need.

**Can I bring my child?**
Yes. Children with permanent teeth follow the same routine, and our pediatric dentistry specialists handle younger cases and anxious children.

**Aftercare.**
You can eat and drink straight away, unless fluoride was applied, in which case wait 30 minutes. Avoid coffee, tea, red wine, dark sauces and tobacco for the rest of the day: the surface takes up stain most readily right after a clean. Brush twice daily with a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste, and clean between the teeth every day. If a tooth is still sensitive after a week, or a gum area keeps bleeding, call us rather than waiting for your recall.
