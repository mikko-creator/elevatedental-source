## §2. What it is

Braces are a bracket bonded to each tooth and a wire threaded through them. The wire wants to return to its own shape. As it does, it carries the teeth with it. Changing the wire changes the force, and changing the force changes what the teeth do next. That is the whole mechanism, and it has not changed in principle for decades. What has changed is the precision with which the target position is planned and the force is sequenced.

Three bracket types are used here. Metal brackets are the smallest and the most efficient at delivering force. Ceramic brackets are tooth-coloured and less visible, and they suit adults who want a fixed appliance without the metal look. Self-ligating brackets hold the wire with a built-in clip rather than an elastic tie, which changes how the wire is engaged.

The problem braces solve is not appearance alone. Crowded teeth trap plaque in places a brush cannot reach, which is why crowded mouths accumulate decay and gum inflammation faster. A deep bite wears the lower incisors down. A crossbite loads teeth sideways in a direction they were not built for. Alignment is a mechanical repair as much as a cosmetic one.

## §3. Who it suits

Braces treat crowding, spacing, rotated teeth, deep bite, open bite, crossbite and a protruding upper arch. They handle severe rotations and large vertical movements more predictably than any removable appliance, which is why complex cases are still treated with fixed appliances.

The honest limits are these.

If you have untreated decay or active gum disease, that is treated before a single bracket is bonded. Bonding brackets over inflamed gums or soft enamel makes both worse for two years. Your assessment will say so.

If your problem is skeletal rather than dental, braces alone will not fix it. A jaw discrepancy in a growing child may be treatable with growth modification if it is caught in time. In an adult with a fully grown jaw, a significant skeletal discrepancy needs orthognathic surgery planned jointly with an oral surgeon. Braces move teeth. They do not move jaws.

If your case is mild to moderate crowding or spacing and being visibly in braces at work is the obstacle, Invisalign (Clear Aligners) is the alternative and it is treated by the same orthodontists here. Clear aligners are less visible and removable for meals. They are also more dependent on you: they only work in the hours you actually wear them. Fixed braces work whether or not you remember them.

If you are not willing to change your brushing routine, do not start. Fixed appliances make cleaning harder for the length of the treatment, and white demineralisation marks around brackets are permanent enamel damage. This is said before treatment, not after.

Children are usually assessed around age 7, when the first permanent molars and incisors are in. Assessment at that age does not mean braces at that age. It means the orthodontist can see whether the arches have room and can time the intervention properly.

## §4. How it works

**1. Consultation and records, 60 to 90 minutes.** Examination, photographs, radiographs, and a digital scan of both arches. Where root position, impacted teeth or the airway need to be assessed in three dimensions, 3D CBCT imaging is taken. Your fee and your likely duration are given to you in writing at this stage.

**2. Diagnosis and treatment plan, 1 to 2 weeks.** The orthodontist sets the target position, decides whether space needs to be created, and decides whether extractions are part of the plan. You see the plan and the reasoning before you agree to it.

**3. Preparatory work, variable.** Cleaning, fillings, gum treatment or extractions are completed first. This is the stage most often skipped elsewhere and most often regretted.

**4. Bonding appointment, 60 to 120 minutes.** Brackets are bonded and the first wire is placed. There is no anaesthesia and no drilling. Expect pressure and soreness for two to five days afterwards.

**5. Adjustments, every 4 to 8 weeks, 15 to 30 minutes each.** Wires are changed in sequence from light and flexible to heavier and more rigid. Elastics may be added. Soreness after each adjustment is briefer than after the first fitting.

**6. Debonding, 60 to 90 minutes.** Brackets and adhesive are removed and the teeth are polished. Most cases complete between 12 and 30 months. Yours depends on how far the teeth have to travel and how consistently you attend.

**7. Retention, indefinite.** A fixed retainer, a removable retainer, or both. Teeth drift back toward where they started. Retention is not the optional epilogue to treatment. It is part of the treatment.

## §5. Who performs it

Braces are planned and adjusted by an orthodontics specialist. The diagnosis, the wire sequence and the decision on extractions are theirs, and the same clinician sees you through the case rather than a different dentist at each adjustment.

Where a case needs a tooth removed to create space, or an impacted canine exposed and bonded, the oral surgeon who does it works in the same practice. Where a case needs restorative work after alignment, so does the prosthodontist. Your case is coordinated inside one clinic rather than posted between three.

## §6. The technology behind it

**3D CBCT imaging.** Available at all four clinics. A flat radiograph shows you a shadow. A CBCT scan shows the root in three dimensions, so an impacted canine can be located precisely, root proximity can be checked before a tooth is moved into a tight space, and root resorption risk can be assessed rather than guessed at.

**iTero intraoral scanner.** Both arches are captured digitally, which removes impression trays from the process and gives a record that can be re-measured later. The same scan supports the clear aligner pathway if you switch to it or finish with it.

**OccluSense bite analysis.** Where the teeth meet at the end of treatment, and how hard, is recorded rather than eyeballed on carbon paper. Alignment that looks correct and loads unevenly is a case that relapses. This is checked before debonding.

Which of these is used depends on your case. A straightforward crowding case does not need a CBCT scan, and you will not be given one to justify the equipment.

Three things move the final figure.

**Case complexity.** A mild alignment case finishing in twelve months and a full case with extractions, elastics and a thirty-month arc are different pieces of work and are priced as such.

**Appliance type.** Metal, ceramic and self-ligating brackets sit at different costs.

**Number of arches and number of visits.** A single-arch case is not priced as a double-arch case, and a case that runs long runs through more adjustment appointments.

The exact figure is confirmed at consultation, after your records have been taken and read. It is given to you in writing before treatment starts, and it states what the figure includes and what it does not.

## §9. FAQ and closing CTA

**How long will I be in braces?**
Most cases run between 12 and 30 months. The orthodontist gives you an estimated range at the planning stage, based on your actual records. Missed appointments extend it, and so does a bracket that keeps coming loose.

**Does it hurt?**
Fitting itself is not painful and needs no anaesthetic. The soreness comes afterwards: pressure and tenderness on biting for two to five days after the fitting, and a shorter, milder version after each adjustment. Soft food and ordinary analgesia handle it. Orthodontic wax covers a bracket that is rubbing.

**Braces or clear aligners?**
It depends on the movements required and on you. Fixed braces handle severe rotations, large vertical corrections and extraction cases more predictably. Clear aligners are less visible and removable, and they suit mild to moderate cases in patients who will wear them 20 to 22 hours a day. The same orthodontists treat both here, so the recommendation is not steered by what the clinic happens to offer.

**What can I not eat?**
Nothing hard, sticky or stringy. No ice, no nuts, no hard bread crusts, no sticky candy, no biting straight into apples or corn on the cob. Cut hard food into pieces. A broken bracket costs you an unscheduled appointment and adds time to the case.

**How do I clean around brackets?**
Brush after every meal, angling the brush above and below the bracket. Use an interdental brush around each bracket and a floss threader or superfloss between the teeth once daily. This is the single behaviour that decides whether you finish with straight teeth or straight teeth with white marks on them.

**Will my teeth move back afterwards?**
Yes, if you stop wearing retainers. Teeth are held in bone that remodels for months after treatment, and the tissue fibres around them keep pulling toward the original position for longer than that. Retainers are worn nightly, indefinitely. That is not a sales line. It is the condition on which the result holds.

**Can I get braces if I have crowns, bridges or implants?**
Crowns and bridges can usually be bracketed, with adjustments to the bonding technique. An implant cannot be moved. It is fused to bone and stays exactly where it was placed, so it becomes a fixed point the plan has to work around. This is why the orthodontist should see you before an implant is placed, not after.

**Aftercare during and after treatment.** Attend every scheduled adjustment. Call the clinic if a bracket comes loose or a wire is poking, rather than waiting for the next appointment. Wear your elastics for the hours you are told, because the mid-treatment correction depends on them entirely. After debonding, wear your retainers exactly as instructed and bring them to every recall so they can be checked for fit and wear.
