Endodontics treats the living tissue inside a tooth. When the pulp is inflamed or infected there are two paths: treat the inside of the tooth, or take the tooth out. Endodontic treatment takes the first path. The infection is cleared, the canal system is sealed, and your own tooth stays in your jaw.

## §2. What this field covers

The pulp is the nerve and blood supply that runs through the center of a tooth. Deep decay, a fracture or a heavy knock can let bacteria reach it. Once that happens the pulp does not recover on its own, and the problem moves down the root towards the bone.

An endodontist treats:

- a toothache that lingers after heat or cold is taken away, or that wakes you at night
- a tooth that is tender to bite on
- an abscess or a swelling in the gum near a root tip
- a tooth that has darkened after an injury
- a previous root canal that has not settled and needs retreatment
- a cracked tooth where the pulp has been exposed
- a tooth that needs the pulp removed before a crown can be built over it

Treatment is carried out under local anesthesia, and the tooth is isolated so the canal stays clean while it is being worked on.

Endodontics is one of the nine fields of dentistry practiced in-house at Elevate Dental. If the tooth needs a crown afterwards, the prosthodontist is in the same clinic. If the tooth turns out to be unrestorable, the oral surgeon is too. You are not sent elsewhere between the diagnosis and the repair.

## §4. The specialists in this field

Root canal work rewards repetition. A molar can carry four or more canals, some of them curved, some of them hidden behind a ledge of dentine, and the shape is not fully known until the tooth is opened. An endodontist works on canal anatomy every day.

That is the case for keeping this in-house. A general practice that meets a difficult molar occasionally sends it to somebody else, and you start again with a clinician who has not met your case. Here the assessment, the treatment and the restoration that follows it stay with one team.

## §5. Technology used here

Endodontics is a field where the imaging decides the plan.

**3D CBCT imaging.** A periapical film is a flat picture of a three-dimensional problem. A CBCT volume shows how many canals a tooth has, how they curve, and how far infection has spread at the root tip, before the tooth is opened. Available at all four clinics.

**Digital radiography.** Digital sensors are used during treatment to confirm working length and to check the final seal. The image appears immediately, so the check happens while you are still in the chair rather than at a second appointment.
