## §2. What this field covers

Four kinds of image are taken here, and each answers a different question.

- **Periapical.** One or two teeth, from the crown to the tip of the root. Used to look for infection at the end of a root and to check the result of a root canal.
- **Panoramic.** Both jaws in a single wide image, including the sinuses and the jaw joints. Used for a broad survey, for wisdom teeth and for planning across a whole arch.
- **Cephalometric.** A standardized side view of the skull. Used in orthodontic planning to measure the relationship between the jaws and the profile they produce.
- **3D CBCT imaging.** A three-dimensional volume of the jaw rather than a flat picture. Used wherever depth matters: implant planning, complex root anatomy, impacted teeth and the position of the inferior alveolar nerve. Available at all four clinics.

What imaging finds:

- decay between teeth and underneath existing fillings
- infection or a cyst at a root tip
- bone loss from gum disease, measured rather than estimated
- impacted and unerupted teeth, wisdom teeth included
- available bone volume and nerve position before an extraction or an implant
- root position and angulation before orthodontic treatment
- the state of an old restoration or a previous root canal

Imaging is one of the nine fields listed on our treatments index, and it is done in-house. Your images are taken, read and used for planning inside the same appointment. Nothing is sent to an outside imaging center, and the reading does not wait on a third party.

## §4. The specialists in this field

An image is only as useful as the clinician reading it. A panoramic image shows a shadow over a root. Whether that shadow is an artifact of the machine, a cyst, or a socket that is healing normally is a judgment call, and the judgment matters more than the picture.

That judgment is made here by the specialist who will treat the case. The endodontist reads the periapical before opening the tooth. The oral surgeon reads the CBCT volume before an extraction near the nerve. The orthodontist reads the cephalometric before deciding how the jaws should relate. The clinician interpreting your scan is the clinician planning your treatment, which is not the case when imaging is bought as a standalone service from an outside center.

Every specialist profile carries a full name, the field that clinician practices and their postnominal credentials, so you can read who will treat you before you book rather than after you arrive.

## §5. Technology used here

On this page the technology is the service, so it is named exactly.

**3D CBCT imaging.** A cone-beam scan producing a three-dimensional volume of the jaw, which can be sliced in any plane and measured in millimeters. It is what implant planning, complex endodontics and impacted-tooth assessment are built on. Available at all four clinics. That four-clinic scope is verified for CBCT specifically.

**Digital radiography.** Direct digital capture in place of film. The image appears in seconds, it can be enlarged and adjusted on screen while you are still in the chair, and the dose is lower than conventional film radiography.

Two further scanning systems sit alongside radiography and are worth separating out, because neither uses radiation.

**iTero intraoral scanner.** Records the surface shape of your teeth and gums as a digital impression. It replaces impression material, not radiography.

**RayFace facial scanner.** Records the proportions of your face in three dimensions, for restorative and smile-design planning.
