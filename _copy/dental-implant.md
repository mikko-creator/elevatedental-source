## §2. What it is

An implant replaces the part of the tooth nobody sees. It is a threaded titanium fixture placed into the jawbone, where bone grows onto its surface and locks it in place. That process is called osseointegration and it takes three to six months. Only after it has happened does the visible part go on.

A finished implant has three components. The fixture in the bone. The abutment, which is the connector that comes through the gum. The crown on top, made in zirconia or lithium disilicate to match the teeth beside it.

The problem it solves is structural, not just cosmetic. When a tooth is lost, the bone that used to hold it starts to shrink, because bone maintains itself in response to load. The teeth either side drift toward the gap and the tooth above or below it drifts down or up into the space. A gap is not a stable situation that stays as it is. An implant reloads the bone and stops the drift, and unlike a bridge it does this without cutting down the healthy teeth on either side.

## §3. Who it suits

Implants suit you if you are missing one tooth, several teeth or a full arch, if your gums are healthy or can be made healthy, and if there is enough bone in the site or enough to build on.

The honest limits matter more here than on almost any other treatment page, because an implant is the largest single restoration most patients ever buy.

If you have active gum disease, it is treated first, without exception. The bacteria that destroy the bone around teeth do the same thing around implants, and the condition is then called peri-implantitis. Placing an implant into an untreated periodontal mouth is placing it into the cause of its own failure.

If there is not enough bone, the site is grafted and left to heal for three to six months before placement. That adds time and cost, and it is disclosed at the plan stage, not discovered later.

If you smoke, your failure risk is higher. This is not a moral point, it is a blood-supply point, and the difference is large enough that it changes the conversation. You will be told the honest position and asked to stop, at least through healing.

If you have uncontrolled diabetes, healing is compromised and the case waits until control improves. If you have taken antiresorptive or bisphosphonate medication, particularly intravenously, tell us before anything is planned, because that history carries a specific risk to the jawbone.

If you are still growing, an implant is premature. It fuses to bone and stays exactly where it was placed while the jaw around it keeps developing. In adolescents, the case waits.

If an implant is not right for you, the alternatives are real ones. A fixed bridge takes weeks rather than months and does not need bone volume, at the cost of preparing the healthy teeth either side. A removable partial denture costs less and can be made quickly, at the cost of stability and comfort. A prosthodontist here can build any of the three, so the recommendation is not shaped by what is on offer.

Implants are also not maintenance-free. They do not decay. The bone around them can still be lost, and it is lost silently. If you will not commit to recall appointments and daily cleaning around the implant, this is the wrong investment.

## §4. How it works

**1. Consultation and 3D CBCT scan, 60 minutes.** Examination, medical history, and a cone beam scan that shows bone height, bone width, bone density, the position of the nerve canal in the lower jaw and the floor of the sinus in the upper. Your case is either straightforward on that scan or it is not, and you are told which at this appointment.

**2. Planning, 1 to 2 weeks.** The surgeon and the restoring clinician agree the implant position before it is placed, working backwards from where the crown has to sit. A crown position decided after the fixture is in the bone is a compromise. Deciding it first is the difference between a restoration that looks correct and one that is merely present.

**3. Preparatory treatment, where needed, 3 to 6 months.** Extraction, socket preservation, bone graft or sinus lift, then healing time. Not every case needs this stage. Cases that do cannot skip it.

**4. Placement, 60 to 90 minutes.** Under local anaesthesia. The site is prepared in sequence and the fixture is placed to a measured torque. Sutures where the technique calls for them. Post-operative swelling and soreness are normal for two to three days.

**5. Osseointegration, 3 to 6 months.** Bone grows onto the fixture. Nothing is rushed here, and this is the stage that a shorter timeline elsewhere is usually shortening. A temporary tooth can be worn during this period in most cases.

**6. Scan and crown fabrication, 2 to 3 weeks.** Once integration is confirmed, the site is scanned and the abutment and crown are made.

**7. Fitting and bite check, 60 to 90 minutes.** The crown is fitted and the bite is checked and adjusted. An implant crown has no periodontal ligament, so it does not have the natural give of a real tooth. Getting the load right at this appointment is what protects it.

**8. Maintenance recall, every 6 months, ongoing.** The tissue around the implant is probed and the bone level is monitored. This appointment is not optional.

## §5. Who performs it

The surgical placement is performed by an oral surgery specialist. The crown that goes on top is made by a prosthodontist. Both work in the same practice, and both agree the plan before the fixture goes in.

That single fact is the reason this treatment reads differently here. In a split arrangement, the surgeon places the implant and someone else has to restore whatever position it ended up in. When the surgeon and the restoring specialist plan together from the same scan, the fixture goes where the crown needs it, not merely where the bone is easiest.

If your case also needs periodontal treatment before placement or orthodontic space opening before the crown, the periodontist and the orthodontist are in the same practice too.

## §6. The technology behind it

**3D CBCT imaging.** Available at all four clinics. For implants this is not a convenience, it is the basis of the plan. The scan measures how much bone you actually have in three dimensions, where the inferior alveolar nerve runs, and where the sinus floor sits. Implant length and diameter are chosen from those measurements. Planning an implant without it is planning from a shadow.

**Piezotome ultrasonic instrumentation.** Where the case involves a sinus lift, a ridge split or a graft, ultrasonic bone cutting is tuned to mineralised tissue and is far less likely to tear the sinus membrane or damage adjacent soft tissue than a rotating bur.

**iTero intraoral scanner.** The site and the opposing arch are captured digitally for the crown, so the fit and the contact points are verified as data before the crown is made.

**OccluSense bite analysis.** An implant crown cannot feel overload the way a natural tooth can, because it has no ligament. OccluSense records where your bite lands and how hard, so the crown can be adjusted out of the heavy contacts rather than left to absorb them.

## §7. What it costs

Dental implants start at ₱90,000 per implant [REPRESENTATIVE-PLACEHOLDER].

Three things move the final figure.

**Case complexity.** A single implant into a healed site with good bone is one procedure. The same tooth needing an extraction, a socket graft, a healing period and then a placement is four appointments and two healing phases.

**Materials.** Fixture system, abutment type and crown material each carry their own cost. A screw-retained zirconia crown and a cemented one are different pieces of work.

**Number of units.** Several implants placed in one surgical visit share one recovery and part of the planning. A full-arch case is quoted as a case, not as a multiple of one tooth.

Ask for the figure to be broken into its stages: surgical placement, any grafting, the abutment, and the crown. A number quoted for one stage alone is not the cost of a finished tooth, and you should know which one you are being given.

The exact figure is confirmed at consultation, after your scan has been read. It is given to you in writing before anything is booked.

## §9. FAQ and closing CTA

**How long does the whole thing take?**
For a straightforward case with good bone, expect four to six months from placement to fitted crown, most of which is healing time you spend doing nothing. A case that needs extraction and grafting first runs longer, commonly nine to twelve months from start to finish.

**Does it hurt?**
Placement is done under local anaesthesia and the surgery itself should not be felt. Afterwards, expect soreness and swelling for two to three days, controlled with prescribed analgesia. Most patients describe the recovery as easier than the extraction that preceded it. Pain that worsens after day three is a reason to call the clinic.

**How long do implants last?**
Published long-term studies of osseointegrated implants report high survival at ten years and beyond, and yours depends heavily on gum health, smoking and whether you keep your recalls. What can be said honestly is this: implants can and do fail, more often through bone loss around them than through anything breaking, and the failure is usually preventable. Nobody can promise you a lifetime, and you should be careful with anyone who does.

**What happens if an implant fails?**
It is removed, the site is allowed to heal, and the case is reassessed. Sometimes a second implant can be placed after grafting. Sometimes a bridge becomes the better answer. What happens to the fee in that situation is a policy question, and it is answered below.

**Can I have the tooth on the same day?**
In selected cases a temporary crown can be fitted at placement. Whether yours qualifies depends on the stability achieved when the fixture is placed and on where the tooth sits in your bite, and that is a decision made in surgery, not in advance. The final crown still waits for integration.

**Implant, bridge or denture?**
An implant preserves the bone and leaves the neighbouring teeth untouched, and it costs the most and takes the longest. A bridge is faster and needs no bone volume, and it requires cutting down two healthy teeth. A removable partial denture is the least invasive and the least stable. A prosthodontist here builds all three and will tell you which one your case actually calls for.

**How do I clean around an implant?**
Brush as normal, and clean under and around the crown daily with an interdental brush, superfloss or a water flosser as directed. Bacteria around an implant behave the way they behave around a tooth, and the bone loss they cause gives no warning until it is advanced. Daily cleaning plus a six-month recall is the whole maintenance requirement.

**Aftercare in the first two weeks.** Ice on and off for the first day. Soft, cool food for several days. No smoking. No rinsing or spitting for the first 24 hours, then warm salt-water rinses. Sleep with your head raised the first night. Take prescribed medication exactly as directed. Avoid chewing on the site until you are told the fixture is integrated. Keep every review appointment.

**Closing.** The first consultation is free. It includes a proper examination and, where your case calls for it, reading your own 3D CBCT scan with the surgeon who would place the implant.
