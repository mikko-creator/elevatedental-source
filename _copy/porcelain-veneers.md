## §2 - What it is

A porcelain veneer is a shell of dental ceramic, typically a few tenths of a millimeter thick, bonded to the visible surface of a tooth. It changes shape, shade, length, width and surface texture in one restoration, and it does it with a material that behaves optically like enamel: light enters it, scatters inside it, and leaves it the way it leaves a natural tooth. That is what separates a veneer that reads as a tooth from one that reads as a covering.

We use Emax, a pressed lithium disilicate ceramic. It is chosen because it is strong enough to be made thin and translucent enough to be layered for a natural edge, which is the combination cosmetic cases need.

Each veneer is made outside the mouth by a laboratory technician working to your design, then bonded to the prepared enamel with a resin cement. The bond between ceramic and enamel is what carries the load, which is why veneers are placed on sound enamel wherever possible and why the preparation is kept conservative.

**The honest part.** Placing porcelain veneers usually requires removing a small amount of enamel so the shell sits flush rather than proud. That removal is irreversible. Once a tooth is prepared it will need a veneer or a crown on it from then on. This is the trade you are making for the durability and stain resistance porcelain gives you, and it is the first thing we discuss, not the last.

## §3 - Who it suits

Porcelain veneers suit healthy teeth with a cosmetic problem that is stable: discoloration that whitening cannot lift, worn or chipped edges, teeth that are too short or too narrow for the smile, small gaps, an uneven edge line, or a mismatched set of front teeth you want brought into proportion.

They suit patients who want the most stain-resistant and hardest-wearing cosmetic option and are prepared to accept irreversible preparation and a laboratory timeline to get it.

**Where they are not the right treatment.**

If you want a conservative option, or you are not ready to commit to enamel reduction, direct composite veneers do a great deal in one visit with little or no cutting. That is the honest alternative and we will say so.

If your teeth are significantly crooked, rotated or crowded, veneers used to fake alignment mean cutting healthy tooth aggressively to fit a shell over a tooth that is in the wrong place. Straighten first with Invisalign (Clear Aligners) or braces, then veneer only what still needs it. The result is better and less tooth is lost.

If you grind or clench, an unprotected ceramic edge can fracture. Grinding is assessed and managed, usually with a night guard, before veneers are considered, not after one breaks.

If a tooth has lost significant structure, has a large existing restoration, or has had root canal treatment, a veneer is covering only one surface of a tooth that needs full support. A crown is the correct restoration in that case.

Active decay and gum disease are treated first. A veneer margin sitting against inflamed tissue will fail, and the tissue will not look right regardless of how good the ceramic is.

## §4 - How it works

1. **Consultation and smile assessment (45 to 60 minutes).** Examination, photographs, and an assessment of your lip line, midline, tooth proportion and gum display. You describe what you want changed; we tell you what porcelain can and cannot do about it, and what it will cost in tooth structure.
2. **Digital records (30 to 45 minutes).** An iTero scan of both arches, a RayFace facial scan, and imaging where the underlying structure needs checking. This is the data the design is built from.
3. **Design and preview (separate visit).** A proposed design is produced and shown to you. Shape, length and shade are discussed and revised at this stage, on screen or in a trial form, while changes are still free.
4. **Preparation (60 to 120 minutes for the set).** Under local anesthetic, a small, measured amount of enamel is removed to the agreed depth. A final scan or impression is taken and sent to the laboratory with your shade prescription.
5. **Temporaries (same appointment).** Temporary veneers are fitted so you leave with functional, presentable teeth. They also let you live with the proposed shape and report back before the ceramic is finished.
6. **Laboratory stage (typically one to two weeks).** The technician builds and finishes each veneer. `[OWNER-DATA]` Confirm the actual laboratory turnaround before publishing a specific number of days.
7. **Try-in and bonding (90 to 120 minutes).** Each veneer is tried in with a trial paste and checked for fit, margin and shade in your own face and lighting. Once you approve, the teeth are isolated and the veneers are bonded and cured.
8. **Bite check and review (30 minutes, plus a follow-up visit).** Occlusion is checked and adjusted, and you return so the gum response and the bite can be reviewed after settling.

Most cases run across two to three clinical visits over two to four weeks, plus the consultation.

## §6 - The technology behind it

**RayFace facial scanner.** A three-dimensional scan of your face. Veneers are designed against your own midline, lip line, smile curve and facial width, which is what stops a technically perfect set of teeth from looking wrong on the person wearing them.

**iTero intraoral scanner.** Digital impressions of both arches without impression material. The scan goes to the laboratory as data, which removes a distortion step between your mouth and the finished ceramic.

**OccluSense bite analysis.** A digital map of where your teeth contact and how hard. New ceramic edges are checked against your real bite, in force as well as position, which is the single most useful thing you can do to protect a veneer from fracturing.

**3D CBCT imaging.** Installed at all four of our clinics. Used where root position, bone level or an existing restoration needs to be understood in three dimensions before a definitive cosmetic plan is committed to.

**Sol Dental Laser.** Used for soft-tissue contouring where the gum line is uneven and the tooth looks short because of the tissue rather than the tooth.

We name the systems because a name can be checked. An adjective cannot.

## §8 - Results

Porcelain holds its shade. Unlike composite, ceramic does not take up pigment from coffee, tea, red wine or tobacco, so a veneer looks in five years much as it looked on the day it was bonded, provided the margin and the gum around it are kept healthy. Veneers are commonly expected to serve for many years before replacement, and the variables are your bite, whether you grind, and how well the margins are maintained.

What porcelain does not do is make the tooth underneath immune to decay. The margin where ceramic meets tooth is the vulnerable line, and it is cleaned like any other tooth surface. Gum health is what keeps that line invisible.

We do not describe your result before we have examined your teeth. Shade, proportion, how much can be changed and what it costs in tooth structure are decided on your case, at the assessment.

## §9 - FAQ and closing CTA

**How long do porcelain veneers last?**
Many years with good maintenance, rather than a fixed term. Bite force, grinding, gum health and how well the margins are cleaned decide it. They are a long-term restoration that will eventually need replacing, and we say that plainly.

**Will my teeth be filed down?**
Usually yes, by a small and measured amount, and that removal cannot be undone. The tooth will need a veneer or a crown from then on. If that is not a trade you want to make, direct composite veneers are the conservative alternative and we will talk you through it.

**Does it hurt?**
Preparation is carried out under local anesthetic. Sensitivity to cold between preparation and bonding, while the temporaries are on, is common and settles once the definitive veneers are in place. Tell your clinician about any sensitivity during that period.

**How many do I need?**
Only as many as the smile needs. Some cases are a single tooth, others span the teeth visible when you smile widely. Treating fewer teeth is better dentistry when fewer teeth is what the case calls for; we will not propose a set of ten to make a plan look complete.

**Can veneers be whitened later?**
No. Ceramic does not respond to whitening gel. If you want a lighter overall shade, whiten your natural teeth first and let the shade settle, then have the veneers made to match. The order matters.

**What happens if one chips or comes off?**
Come in. A debonded veneer can often be re-bonded if it is intact; a fractured one is generally remade rather than repaired, because ceramic does not patch the way composite does. `[OWNER-DATA]` Replacement and remake terms are pending the client's written policy.

**Porcelain or composite?**
Porcelain: harder, holds its shade, laboratory-made, two to three visits, requires irreversible preparation, higher starting price. Composite: one visit, conservative, repairable chairside, lower starting price, stains and chips more readily. The right answer depends on your teeth, your bite, your timeline and how much tooth structure you are willing to give up.

**Aftercare.**
Treat your temporaries carefully: soft food, no flossing straight up and out at the temporary margin, and call us if one comes loose rather than waiting. Once the definitive veneers are bonded, clean them exactly as you would enamel, with daily flossing at the margins, because gum health at that line is what keeps the result invisible. Do not use your front teeth as tools. No ice, no nails, no opening packaging. If you grind at night, wear the guard every night. Keep your recall appointments so margins and gum levels are checked before a small problem becomes a remake.
