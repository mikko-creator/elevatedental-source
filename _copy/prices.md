## §2 - How our pricing works

We charge per treatment. You pay for the treatment you have, at the fee confirmed for your case, and nothing is bundled into a plan you did not ask for.

Every figure on this page is a starting price. It is the point at which that treatment begins, for a straightforward case, in the standard material. It is not a quote. Your own fee is set once a specialist has examined you and reviewed your imaging, and it is given to you in writing before any work starts.

Elevate Dental does not accept HMOs or dental insurance. All treatment is self-pay. HMO coverage cannot be used for dental treatments at our clinics.

## §4 - What changes the final fee

A starting price is honest only if you are told what moves it. Four things do.

**Case complexity.** A crown on a sound tooth and a crown on a tooth that first needs root canal treatment are not the same piece of work. The starting price covers the treatment itself. Any preparatory treatment your case needs is quoted as its own line.

**Material choice.** Veneers, crowns and bridges are made in more than one material. Emax porcelain, zirconia and direct composite differ in strength, translucency and stain resistance, and they are priced differently. Your specialist sets out which materials suit your case, and what each one costs, before you choose.

**Number of units.** Most restorative and cosmetic work is priced per tooth, per unit or per arch. One veneer is one unit. A ten-unit smile design is quoted as ten units plus the design work the case requires. Every row in the tables above says which unit its starting price refers to.

**Preparatory work.** Some cases need imaging, an extraction, gum treatment or bone preparation before the treatment you came in for can begin. Where that is true, it appears on your written quote before you agree to anything, not afterwards.

Nothing is charged at the desk that was not on the quote you agreed to.

## §5 - We do not accept HMO or dental insurance

Elevate Dental does not accept HMOs or dental insurance. All treatment is self-pay. HMO coverage cannot be used for dental treatments at our clinics.

This is worth stating plainly on the page where you are working out what treatment will cost, because it changes the arithmetic. There is no card to present at the desk and no claim to file afterwards. The figure on your written quote is the figure you settle.

What you get in return is a fee set by your case and by nobody else, quoted in writing before treatment starts, with no third party deciding which treatment or which material you are entitled to.

If you are used to dental care being handled through a provider, raise it at your consultation. Our team will walk you through what your treatment costs and how it is settled, before you commit to anything.

## §8 - Start with a free consultation

If a starting price is higher than you expected, the next step is not to walk away from it. It is to find out what your case actually needs.

The consultation is free. A specialist examines you, takes the imaging your case calls for, and tells you what is wrong, what the options are, and which of them you can reasonably defer. You leave with a written quote covering each treatment discussed and the order it would happen in.

Nothing is booked at that appointment. You take the quote away and decide.

## §9 - Why we publish starting prices

A patient deciding whether to treat a broken tooth or straighten a bite is deciding about money as much as about dentistry. Being made to book a consultation simply to find out whether a treatment is within reach is a poor way to begin a clinical relationship, and it wastes the patient's morning.

So we publish where every treatment begins. A starting price is not a quote, and this page has never pretended otherwise. It is enough information to decide whether to take the next step, which is the only decision you are being asked to make right now.

The figure quoted after your examination may be higher than the starting price. If it is, you will be told why, in writing, before anything begins.
