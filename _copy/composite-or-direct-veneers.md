## §2 - What it is

A direct veneer is composite resin built up on the visible surface of a tooth and shaped in the mouth. The same tooth-colored material used for fillings is applied in thin layers, each set with a curing light, then contoured and polished until it reads as enamel rather than as a covering.

The word direct is the whole distinction. A porcelain veneer is made outside the mouth by a laboratory technician and bonded on at a second visit. A direct veneer is made in the chair by the clinician, in one sitting, with the result in front of you the entire time. That means shape, length, edge translucency and shade are adjusted while you watch, not approved from a model.

It also means the treatment is conservative. Where the tooth simply needs to be widened, lengthened or resurfaced, composite can often be added with little or no enamel removed. Enamel that is never cut is enamel you still have if you decide to change course later.

Composite is used to close a small gap between the front teeth, rebuild a chipped edge, mask a discolored patch, correct a tooth that sits slightly short or narrow, and even out an irregular edge line across the smile.

## §3 - Who it suits

Direct veneers suit a defined, mostly cosmetic problem on healthy teeth: a chip, a small gap, an uneven edge, a single tooth out of shade with its neighbors, a peg-shaped lateral incisor. They suit patients who want a change completed in one appointment, and patients who would rather not commit to irreversible tooth reduction yet.

They are also a considered choice for younger patients, where the gum line will still change with age and a conservative restoration that can be adjusted or replaced is more sensible than a definitive one.

**Where they are not the right treatment.**

Composite is softer than porcelain. It stains at the margins over time, particularly with coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco, and it loses surface gloss and needs repolishing. If you want the most stain-resistant and hardest-wearing option, that is porcelain veneers, and the honest trade is cost and irreversibility against durability.

If you grind or clench, an unprotected composite edge will chip. That case needs the grinding addressed, usually with a night guard, before or alongside any cosmetic work.

If the teeth are significantly crooked, rotated or crowded, veneering over the misalignment is the wrong instrument. Composite added to a tooth that is out of position makes it bulky rather than straight. Orthodontic treatment first, with Invisalign (Clear Aligners) or braces, then a small amount of composite to finish, gives a result that a veneer alone cannot.

If the discoloration is deep and intrinsic, such as tetracycline banding or a darkened non-vital tooth, thin composite may not mask it. Porcelain, or internal whitening of the non-vital tooth, is assessed instead.

Active decay and untreated gum disease are treated first. Composite bonds to sound enamel, not to a compromised surface.

## §4 - How it works

1. **Consultation and smile assessment (30 to 45 minutes).** Your teeth are photographed and assessed against your lip line, midline and facial proportions. You say what you want changed in your own words; we say what is achievable with composite and what is not.
2. **Digital records and design (20 to 30 minutes, same or separate visit).** A digital scan and a facial scan are taken so the proposed shape is planned against your face rather than against a textbook proportion.
3. **Shade selection (about 10 minutes).** Shade is chosen before the teeth are isolated, because teeth dehydrate and lighten under isolation and a shade picked late will read wrong.
4. **Preparation (10 to 20 minutes).** Often minimal. The enamel surface is cleaned and conditioned; where a small amount of reduction is genuinely needed to avoid a bulky result, it is discussed and agreed before it is done.
5. **Layering and sculpting (30 to 60 minutes per tooth).** Composite is placed and light-cured in layers, with different shades and opacities used to reproduce the way a natural tooth transmits light at the body and at the edge.
6. **Bite check (about 10 minutes).** Occlusion is checked and adjusted, particularly on the biting edges of the upper front teeth, which is where an unadjusted veneer chips first.
7. **Contouring and polish (20 to 30 minutes).** Shape is refined and the surface is polished through progressive grits. Polish is what makes composite look like enamel and what slows staining.

One to two teeth are usually completed in a single appointment of one to two hours. A full set of upper front teeth is normally staged across more than one visit, because sculpting six or eight teeth well cannot be rushed.

## §6 - The technology behind it

**RayFace facial scanner.** A three-dimensional scan of your face. Veneer proportions are designed against your actual midline, lip line and facial width, so the result is planned in the context it will be seen in.

**iTero intraoral scanner.** A digital scan of the arch, taken without impression material. It records the starting position, supports the design stage, and gives a reference to compare the finished work against.

**OccluSense bite analysis.** A digital map of where and how hard your teeth contact. Used to check the new edges against your real bite, which is what protects a composite edge from chipping.

**Sol Dental Laser.** Where the gum line is uneven and the tooth looks short because of the tissue rather than the tooth, soft-tissue contouring is carried out with the laser before the composite is placed.

**3D CBCT imaging.** Installed at all four of our clinics, and used where the underlying root or bone position needs to be understood before a cosmetic plan is committed to.

## §8 - Results

A well-executed direct veneer restores the shape and edge of the tooth in one appointment and is finished, polished and in function before you leave. Composite veneers are commonly expected to serve for a number of years before they need repolishing, repair or replacement, and how long yours serve depends on your bite, whether you grind, and how much coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco the margins are exposed to.

They are repairable. A chipped edge on a composite veneer is usually rebuilt chairside, which is not true of porcelain. That repairability is a genuine advantage of the material and part of why it is chosen.

We will not tell you what your result will look like before we have seen your teeth. Shade, edge position and how much can be changed on your specific case are decided at the assessment.

## §9 - FAQ and closing CTA

**How long do composite veneers last?**
A number of years, with maintenance, rather than a fixed figure. The variables are your bite force, whether you grind, and stain exposure. They are designed to be repolished and repaired over their life rather than replaced wholesale.

**Will my tooth be filed down?**
Often very little, and in some cases not at all. Where a small reduction genuinely improves the result, it is explained and agreed before it happens. This is the main reason patients choose direct veneers over porcelain.

**Do they stain?**
Composite takes up stain at the margins over time, more readily than porcelain does. Coffee, tea, red wine, dark sauces and tobacco accelerate it. Polishing at your recall visit removes most surface staining.

**Can they be whitened later?**
No. Composite does not respond to whitening gel. If you want a lighter overall shade, whiten your natural teeth first and let the shade settle, then match the veneers to the new shade. Doing it in that order is the difference between a matched result and a mismatched one.

**How do they compare to porcelain veneers?**
Composite: one visit, conservative, repairable, lower starting price, more prone to stain and chip. Porcelain: two or three visits, requires irreversible enamel reduction, harder and more stain-resistant, higher starting price. Neither is superior in the abstract. Your teeth, your bite and what you want changed decide it.

**Does it hurt?**
Where no enamel is removed, anesthetic is usually not needed at all. Where a small reduction is planned, local anesthetic is offered. Tell your clinician if any part of the appointment is uncomfortable and it is adjusted.

**Can I have just one tooth done?**
Yes, and single-tooth matching is the harder skill of the two. The composite has to reproduce the neighboring tooth's shade, translucency and surface texture, which is why shade selection and layering take the time they do.

**Aftercare.**
Avoid coffee, tea, red wine, curry and tobacco for the first 48 hours while the surface fully matures. After that, treat the veneers as you would enamel, with one exception: do not use them as tools. No opening packaging, no biting nails, no chewing ice or pens. Brush with a non-abrasive toothpaste, floss daily around the margins, and if you grind at night, wear the guard. Come back at your recall so the surfaces can be repolished before staining sets into the margin.
