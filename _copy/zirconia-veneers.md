## §2. What it is

A zirconia veneer is a shell of zirconium dioxide, milled from a solid ceramic block and bonded to the prepared front surface of a tooth. It is one of the two hard-ceramic options in cosmetic dentistry. The other is lithium disilicate, sold as Emax and used on our porcelain veneer cases.

The difference between the two is the point of this page. Zirconia is the denser and more fracture-resistant of the two, and it is more opaque. Opacity is not a flaw here. It is the reason zirconia is specified. A tooth that has gone grey after root canal treatment, a tooth banded by tetracycline staining, a tooth with a dark metal core underneath: these show through a translucent ceramic. Zirconia covers them.

Zirconia does not absorb pigment the way composite does. Coffee, tea, red wine and tobacco stain the polished surface far less readily than they stain a directly bonded resin. That is the second reason it gets specified.

The problem zirconia veneers solve is a specific one. You have front teeth that are discoloured, chipped, worn, slightly rotated or unevenly sized, the discolouration is deep rather than surface-level, and you want the correction to hold up to a strong bite.

## §3. Who it suits

Zirconia veneers suit you if you have deep intrinsic discolouration that whitening cannot lift, if you have worn or chipped edges on front teeth, if you have small gaps or minor rotations you do not want to spend two years correcting orthodontically, or if you grind and a more translucent ceramic has already failed you.

They are not for everyone, and the honest limits are these.

If you have active decay or gum disease, that is treated first. A veneer bonded over an unstable foundation is a wasted veneer. Your assessment will say so plainly.

If your teeth are significantly out of alignment, veneers are the wrong instrument. Moving teeth is orthodontics. Covering them is cosmetics. Ten ceramic units used to disguise a bite problem is a bigger, more expensive and less stable answer than braces or Invisalign (Clear Aligners) followed by two or three veneers, and our orthodontists will tell you that before our cosmetic dentists prepare anything.

If you grind heavily and will not wear a nightguard, no ceramic option is a sound investment. Zirconia is strong. It is not indestructible, and neither is the tooth under it.

If the tooth has lost most of its structure, a veneer does not have enough left to bond to. A crown is the correct restoration in that case, and you will be told so rather than sold the more cosmetic-sounding option.

If your priority is the light-scattering translucency of a natural upper central incisor and your underlying tooth colour is already good, Emax porcelain will usually read more naturally than zirconia. Both are on the same menu here, which is why the recommendation can be made on the merits.

Zirconia veneers are also irreversible. A layer of enamel is removed to make room for the shell, usually between half a millimetre and a millimetre on the facing surface. Once that enamel is gone the tooth is committed to being restored. This is stated at the consultation, not in the small print.

## §4. How it works

**1. Consultation and records, 60 to 90 minutes.** Examination, photographs, a digital scan of your teeth, and radiographs where they are needed to check the health of the teeth involved. Your shade, your bite and your gum line are all recorded. You are given the fee for your case in writing at this stage.

**2. Smile design, 1 to 2 weeks.** Your case is designed against the proportions of your face, not against a chart. You see the proposed shape and length before anything is cut.

**3. Trial smile, 45 to 60 minutes.** The design is transferred into your mouth in temporary material so you can look at it, speak with it and take it home to think about. Changes made at this appointment are free. Changes made after the ceramic is milled are not.

**4. Preparation and temporaries, 2 to 3 hours.** Under local anaesthesia, the facing surfaces are prepared and a final scan is taken. You leave with temporary veneers that match the approved design.

**5. Laboratory fabrication, 1 to 2 weeks.** The units are milled, characterised and glazed.

**6. Fitting and bonding, 1 to 2 hours.** The veneers are tried in, checked against your face and your bite, then bonded. Your bite is adjusted at the same visit.

**7. Review, 1 to 2 weeks later, 20 minutes.** The gums are checked as they settle around the new margins, and the bite is confirmed a second time once the anaesthetic memory has worn off and you are chewing normally.

## §6. The technology behind it

Three named systems carry a veneer case here.

**RayFace facial scanner.** We use a RayFace 3D facial scanner to plan veneers, crowns and smile design against the proportions of your actual face. Tooth width, midline and incisal edge position are set against your lip line and your facial midline rather than against a generic template.

**iTero intraoral scanner.** Your preparations and your opposing teeth are captured digitally instead of in impression putty. The scan goes to the laboratory as data, so the fit is checked before the ceramic is milled rather than after.

**OccluSense bite analysis.** Where your bite is a factor, and with veneers on worn or ground-down teeth it usually is, OccluSense records where and how hard your teeth meet. Contact points that would load a new veneer edge unevenly are found and adjusted at the fitting appointment.

Zoom whitening is also available here, and it is relevant to sequencing rather than to the veneers themselves. Ceramic does not respond to bleach. If you intend to whiten your remaining natural teeth, that is done before the veneer shade is chosen, so the ceramic is matched to the colour you are going to keep.

Three things move the final figure.

**Case complexity.** A single veneer replacing a chipped edge is not the same job as a ten-unit upper design that has to re-establish your midline and incisal plane.

**Material and finish.** Monolithic zirconia, layered zirconia and a hand-characterised anterior unit sit at different laboratory costs.

**Number of units.** The design work is shared across the case, so the per-tooth figure behaves differently for two teeth than for eight. The number that matters to you is the total for your case, and that is what you are given.

The exact figure is confirmed at consultation, after your teeth have been examined and scanned. It is given to you in writing before any preparation is booked.

## §9. FAQ and closing CTA

**How long do zirconia veneers last?**
Ceramic veneers are commonly quoted with a service life in the region of ten to fifteen years. Yours will depend on three things you control: whether you grind, whether you wear a nightguard if you do, and how well you keep the margins clean. Plan for eventual replacement rather than treating them as a one-time purchase.

**Does it hurt?**
Preparation is done under local anaesthesia and you should not feel the work itself. Expect some sensitivity to cold and to pressure for a few days between preparation and fitting, while you are in temporaries. Ordinary analgesia handles it. Tell us if it does not.

**Will they look fake?**
That is a design question, not a material question. The shade, the length, the edge translucency and the width-to-height ratio are all decided at the trial smile stage, in your mouth, before anything is milled. If it looks wrong at that appointment, it gets changed at that appointment.

**Can zirconia veneers stain?**
The glazed ceramic surface resists staining well. The bonding margin at the gum line is a different surface and can pick up stain if it is not cleaned properly. This is one of the reasons the six-month recall matters.

**Do I still need to floss?**
Yes, and more carefully than before. The veneer does not decay. The tooth underneath it does, and the margin where the two meet is exactly where decay starts. Floss the margins daily.

**Can a zirconia veneer be repaired if it chips?**
Zirconia does not repair the way composite does. A chipped unit is normally replaced. That is a reason to take the nightguard advice seriously.

**What if I do not like the result?**
The point of the trial smile is that you approve the shape before the tooth is prepared and before the ceramic is made. That is your decision point, and it is deliberately placed before the irreversible step. Once the ceramic is milled to an approved design, changes mean remaking units at cost.
