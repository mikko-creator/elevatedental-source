## §2 - What it is

Plaque is not a coating you can scrub off in one pass. It is a biofilm: a living, organized layer of bacteria that binds itself to enamel and to the root surface just under the gum line, and rebuilds within hours of being disturbed. Left alone it hardens into calculus, and calculus is what inflames gums.

AirFlow is the instrument built for that specific problem. A handpiece delivers warm water, compressed air and a fine powder in one jet. The powder is soft enough to be used on enamel, on exposed root surfaces, on implant necks and around orthodontic brackets, and the jet reaches the pits, fissures and interdental spaces that a rubber cup cannot enter.

Your appointment starts with a disclosing solution that stains the biofilm a visible color. You can see exactly where it sits, and so can your dentist. That is the guided part of guided biofilm therapy: nothing is treated by guesswork, and at the end of the session you are shown the same surfaces again. Surface stain from coffee, tea, red wine, soy and tobacco comes away with the biofilm.

Where hardened calculus is present, AirFlow alone will not remove it. Ultrasonic scaling is added in the same visit for those deposits. The two are used together, not as alternatives.

## §3 - Who it suits

AirFlow suits you if you keep a regular cleaning schedule and want the maintenance visit done thoroughly and quickly. It is particularly useful if you wear fixed braces, a retainer or a bridge, if you have implants, or if you have exposed root surfaces that make conventional polishing uncomfortable.

It is also the right choice if your concern is stain rather than shade. Coffee, tea and tobacco marks sit on the surface and come away.

**Where it is not the right treatment.**

AirFlow does not whiten teeth. It removes what is sitting on top of the enamel and returns the tooth to its own natural shade. If you want to go lighter than your natural shade, that is teeth whitening, and it is a separate treatment.

If your gums bleed on brushing, if pockets have formed, or if bone loss has already begun, a maintenance clean is not the correct treatment. That case belongs with our periodontics specialists, and the treatment is tooth scaling and root planing rather than a routine clean. We will tell you that at the assessment rather than after the fact.

If you have untreated decay, the cavity is restored first. Cleaning around active decay does not stop it.

Tell us before we start if you have asthma or another chronic respiratory condition, if you are on a sodium-restricted diet, if you are pregnant, or if you have a recent surgical site in the mouth. Any of these changes the powder, the pressure or the technique, and in some cases we will use hand and ultrasonic instruments instead.

## §4 - How it works

1. **Consultation and assessment (10 to 15 minutes).** Your dentist reviews your medical history, examines the soft tissue, and charts gum condition and pocket depths. This is where a routine clean is separated from periodontal treatment.
2. **Disclosing (about 5 minutes).** A disclosing solution is applied so the biofilm becomes visible. You are shown it before anything is removed.
3. **AirFlow biofilm removal (10 to 20 minutes).** The handpiece works surface by surface, including the interdental spaces and the gum margin. Powder, water temperature and pressure are set to your sensitivity.
4. **Ultrasonic scaling where required (5 to 15 minutes).** Hardened calculus above and just below the gum margin is removed with an ultrasonic tip.
5. **Finish and check (about 5 minutes).** Surfaces are rechecked with the disclosing agent, and fluoride is applied where it is indicated.
6. **Home care and recall (about 5 minutes).** You leave with a specific home routine for the areas that were holding biofilm, and a recall interval set to your case rather than a default.

Most appointments run 45 to 60 minutes end to end. A first visit, or a visit after a long gap, runs longer.

## §9 - FAQ and closing CTA

**Does it hurt?**
Most patients describe it as a cool spray with light pressure. Exposed root surfaces can be sensitive. Tell your dentist as it happens and the powder, the water temperature and the pressure are adjusted for you.

**How is this different from a regular clean?**
Oral prophylaxis is the routine scale and polish. AirFlow is a specific instrument aimed at biofilm and surface stain, guided by a disclosing agent so treatment follows what is actually there. In many appointments both are used together.

**How often should I have it?**
Most patients are recalled every six months. If you smoke, wear fixed braces, have implants, or have a history of gum disease, your dentist may set a shorter interval. The interval is set to your case at the end of the appointment.

**Will it whiten my teeth?**
No. It removes surface stain and returns the tooth to its natural shade. Going lighter than your natural shade is teeth whitening, which is a different treatment.

**Is it safe with braces, implants, crowns or veneers?**
Yes, and it is often the preferred instrument around them, because the powder jet reaches into places a rubber cup cannot and does not scratch restorative surfaces the way an abrasive paste can.

**Do I need X-rays first?**
Not for a routine clean. If your gum assessment shows pocketing or suspected bone loss, imaging is taken so the periodontal picture is measured rather than assumed. 3D CBCT imaging is available at all four clinics.

**Can I eat and drink straight after?**
Yes. For the first few hours, avoid coffee, tea, red wine, dark sauces and tobacco: the enamel surface is at its most receptive to stain immediately after a clean.

**Aftercare.**
Brush twice daily with a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste. Clean between the teeth every day, with floss or interdental brushes, and give particular attention to the areas your dentist showed you were holding biofilm. Avoid staining foods, drinks and tobacco for the first few hours. If a tooth stays sensitive after a week, or a gum area bleeds persistently, call us rather than waiting for your recall.
