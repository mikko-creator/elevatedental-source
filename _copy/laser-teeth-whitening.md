## §2 - What it is

Tooth color has two components. Surface stain sits on top of the enamel and comes off with a professional clean. Intrinsic color is inside the tooth, in the dentine beneath the enamel, and no amount of cleaning reaches it. Whitening treats the second one.

The gel is a peroxide compound. It penetrates the enamel and breaks the larger pigmented molecules held in the dentine into smaller ones that reflect more light, so the tooth reads lighter. Nothing is removed from the tooth and no layer is added to it. The structure is unchanged; the color of what is already there is altered.

Zoom is a light-activated system. A controlled light source is directed at the gel to accelerate the reaction across all the treated teeth at once, in timed cycles, with your gums, lips and cheeks isolated behind a protective barrier the entire time.

**One point of precision.** This page sits at a URL that says laser, and the treatment is not a laser treatment. Zoom is light-activated. We use the accurate term because the difference is real, and because a clinic that is loose with the small facts is not a clinic to trust with the large ones.

In-clinic whitening is supervised whitening. The gel is stronger than anything sold over a counter, which is exactly why it is applied by a clinician, behind a barrier, on a mouth that has been examined first.

## §3 - Who it suits

Whitening suits you if your teeth are healthy and your gums are healthy, and your natural shade has yellowed with age, coffee, tea, red wine, soy or tobacco. Yellow and orange-brown shades generally respond better than gray ones.

It suits patients preparing for a specific event, and patients who intend to have cosmetic work later: whitening first, then matching the restorations to the new shade, is the correct sequence.

**Where it is not the right treatment.**

Whitening gel does not change the color of any material that is not tooth. Crowns, veneers, bridges, composite fillings and bonded edges stay exactly the shade they were made. If you have a restoration on a front tooth, whitening the teeth around it will make that restoration stand out, and it will need replacing to match afterwards. We will show you this on your own teeth before you decide, not afterwards.

Deep intrinsic discoloration, such as tetracycline banding, responds poorly and sometimes unevenly. A single dark non-vital tooth is a different problem with a different treatment, usually internal whitening of that tooth alone. Fluorosis, white spot lesions and dark cracks each behave differently under gel, and are assessed individually.

Untreated decay, leaking restorations, exposed dentine and active gum disease are treated before any whitening, not through it. Peroxide reaching an unsealed cavity or inflamed tissue causes real pain.

We do not whiten during pregnancy or while nursing, and we do not whiten patients under 18 without a specific clinical assessment.

If sensitivity is your main concern, say so at the consultation. It changes how we plan the session rather than whether you can have one.

## §4 - How it works

1. **Consultation and examination (20 to 30 minutes).** Decay check, gum check, and a review of every existing restoration on a visible tooth. Your starting shade is recorded against a shade guide and photographed, so the change is measured rather than remembered.
2. **Professional clean beforehand (separate appointment).** Whitening is carried out on clean teeth. Surface deposit blocks the gel unevenly, and an uneven start produces an uneven result. Most patients have oral prophylaxis first.
3. **Isolation and protection (15 to 20 minutes).** A retractor holds the lips and cheeks clear, a barrier is placed and cured along the gum line, and the soft tissue is sealed off before any gel is dispensed. This step is most of the reason in-clinic whitening is safe at this concentration.
4. **Gel application and light cycles (about 45 to 60 minutes).** The gel is applied to the front teeth and activated in timed cycles, typically three or four of about 15 minutes each, with fresh gel between cycles. You rest through it.
5. **Removal, desensitizer and final shade (15 to 20 minutes).** The gel and barrier are removed, a desensitizing agent is applied, and your finishing shade is recorded against the same guide and photographed alongside the start.
6. **Aftercare briefing (about 10 minutes).** The first 48 hours matter more than the rest of the month, and you leave knowing exactly why.

Allow about 90 minutes to two hours in the clinic for the whitening session itself, plus the earlier consultation and clean.

## §6 - The technology behind it

**Zoom whitening.** The named in-clinic system used here. It is light-activated, not laser, and it treats the full front-of-mouth in timed cycles under direct supervision.

**AirFlow (guided biofilm therapy).** Used beforehand where surface stain and biofilm are heavy, so the gel meets clean enamel and works evenly across every tooth.

**iTero intraoral scanner.** A digital record of the arch, used where whitening is the first stage of a restorative or cosmetic plan and the shade decision has to carry through to laboratory work.

**RayFace facial scanner.** Used when whitening is planned alongside veneers or crowns, so the target shade is judged against your face rather than against a swatch held in isolation.

We name the systems because a name is checkable. An adjective is not.

## §8 - Results

Your shade is recorded at the start and at the end of the session against the same guide, and photographed under the same conditions, so what changed is documented rather than described. How much lighter your teeth go depends on the type and depth of your discoloration, your enamel thickness and your starting shade. We will not put a number of shades on your result before we have examined your teeth, and no clinic honestly can.

The shade will relapse gradually. Teeth take up color from what passes over them every day, so coffee, tea, red wine, dark sauces and tobacco will move the shade back toward where it started. How fast depends entirely on your habits. Many patients maintain with take-home trays or a repeat session at intervals, planned with your clinician.

Sensitivity to cold during the 24 to 48 hours after a session is common and expected. A desensitizing agent is applied at the end of the appointment to reduce it, and it typically settles on its own. Tell us if it does not.

## §9 - FAQ and closing CTA

**How white will my teeth get?**
That depends on your starting shade and the type of discoloration, and it is assessed on your own teeth at the consultation. Yellow tones generally lift more readily than gray ones. Anyone quoting you a specific number of shades before looking in your mouth is guessing.

**How long does it last?**
It fades gradually rather than ending on a date. Coffee, tea, red wine, dark sauces and tobacco are the main drivers. With careful habits and maintenance the result holds considerably longer than with none.

**Does it damage enamel?**
The gel works by altering pigment inside the tooth, not by removing tooth structure. It is used at clinical concentration, behind a gum barrier, on a mouth that has been examined first, and that supervision is the difference between whitening and harm.

**Will my crowns, veneers and fillings whiten too?**
No. Restorative materials do not respond to whitening gel. If you have visible restorations, whitening the natural teeth around them will change how well they match, and they may need replacing afterwards. This is discussed and priced before you start, not after.

**Is it uncomfortable?**
Cold sensitivity and occasional short zings during and after the session are common, and a desensitizing agent is applied at the end. Tell us at the consultation if your teeth are already sensitive, and the protocol is adjusted for you.

**Can I whiten at home instead?**
Take-home trays fitted to your own impressions are a legitimate option, and a slower one. Generic strips and counter kits use lower concentrations, are not shaped to your teeth, and can irritate the gum where the gel spreads. We will tell you which route suits your case.

**How soon before a wedding or an event should I book?**
Allow at least two weeks. That leaves room for the clean beforehand, for sensitivity to settle, for the shade to stabilize, and for any restoration that needs rematching to be dealt with without hurry.

**Aftercare.**
For the first 48 hours the enamel is at its most receptive to pigment, so keep to pale food and drink: water, milk, plain rice, chicken, white fish, potato. Avoid coffee, tea, cola, red wine, soy sauce, curry, beetroot, berries and tobacco entirely in that window. Use a desensitizing toothpaste if cold bothers you. Rinse with water after coffee or wine from then on, and keep your recall appointments so surface stain is removed before it sets.
